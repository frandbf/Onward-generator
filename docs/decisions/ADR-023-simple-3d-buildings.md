# ADR-023 — OSM Simple 3D Buildings tags: parts, roof shapes and colour tints

Status: accepted · 31 October 2026. Realism study opportunity #1 (`docs/plans/2026-10-30-realism-sources.md`). Amends [ADR-004](ADR-004-facade-detail.md) (roofs and parts) and [ADR-009](ADR-009-dress-materials.md) (materials). It lands in three pull requests: roofs (decisions 1–5), then parts (6), then colour tints (7).

## Context

OSM carries a lot of 3D tagging under the [Simple 3D Buildings](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Simple_3D_Buildings) scheme, and `detail` threw most of it away:

- A `roof:shape` became the centroid fan (`hip`) and only on a footprint of at most 800 m². Every shape looked the same, and `roof:height`, `roof:levels`, `roof:direction` and `roof:orientation` were ignored.
- `building:colour` and `roof:colour` were ignored.
- No `building:part` was ever drawn. `geo` keeps a part way as a closed line, which `detail` did not read as a polygon, and a part with no `height` was dropped (`building_part_no_height`) even when it had `building:levels`.

Madrid's OSM extract alone has 19 678 `building:part` ways, 23 779 buildings with `building:levels`, 3 163 `roof:levels`, 178 `roof:height`, 1 625 `roof:shape`, 449 `building:colour` and 351 `roof:colour`. Old towns in central Europe (Kraków) are tagged more densely still.

## Decision

The rules come from the scheme's wiki description. The ideas follow what OSM2World and Streets GL do (both MIT). No code was copied from them, and nothing comes from GPL tools (blosm, roofer).

1. **Roofs are height fields** (`detail/roofs.py`). A roof is a function z(x, y) over the footprint. The footprint is cut along the lines where the slope changes (the ridge and the hips), each piece is triangulated flat and lifted onto the field, and every wall edge the roof leaves above the eaves gets a vertical gable piece in the wall material. The pieces keep the footprint's own vertices, so the shell stays watertight without new seams, and the cost grows with the vertex count (a 12 × 8 m gable is 4 + 2 triangles).
2. **Shapes.** `gabled`, `hipped`, `half-hipped`, `skillion`, `pyramidal` and `dome` are drawn. A hipped or half-hipped roof needs a footprint that fills at least 95 % of its minimum rotated rectangle; on any other footprint it becomes a pyramid or a gable, and the record says so. Every other value is drawn as the nearest of those six (`mansard` → hipped; `gambrel`, `saltbox`, `round` → gabled; `onion` → dome; `cone` → pyramidal) and recorded as an `approximation`. A half-hipped end is a gable up to half the rise and a hip above it, with every face at the same pitch. The dome is three rings on a quarter circle. A real onion profile was not worth its triangles at headset distance.
3. **Rise and direction.** The rise is `roof:height`, else `roof:levels` × the building's storey height, else `roof:angle` over the half span, else 30° capped at 4 m (`rise_origin: estimated`). The ridge runs along the long side of the minimum rotated rectangle, across it with `roof:orientation=across`, or square to `roof:direction`.
4. **Fallback.** When the shape does not fit, the roof is the template's flat roof, and the reason goes into `roof.fallback` and a `roof_shape_fallback` issue (info). The cases are a courtyard, several outer rings, more than 64 vertices, less than 1 m across the slope, a rise under 0.1 m, and a centroid that cannot see every eave (pyramid, dome). An unknown value keeps the template roof and gets `roof_shape_unsupported`. No estimated hip is drawn over a tag.
5. **Height stays phase 03's.** The roof sits on the building's recorded height, as the old hip did, because phase 03 extrudes that height and its collider is that prism.
6. **Parts.** A `building:part` (closed line or polygon) is clipped to the building it overlaps most. Its height is `height`, else `(building:levels + roof:levels)` × the storey height `heights` uses for that building's category (`height.origin: estimated`, with `rule` written out). Its base is `min_height`, else `building:min_level` × the storey height. A part no higher than its building lies inside the building's walls and is skipped (`building_part_hidden`). A part's roof sits under its `height` (S3DB: `height` includes the roof), and the rise is cut so at least 0.5 m of wall remains. The collider runs from the base to the eaves.
7. **Colours are tints, not textures.** `building:colour` and `roof:colour` (a CSS name or `#rgb`/`#rrggbb`) are quantized in CIELAB to a fixed palette: 12 wall tints (white, cream, yellow, ochre, orange, pink, red, brown, grey, dark, green, blue) and 7 roof tints (red, brown, grey, dark, light, green, blue). A tinted material is `<base>__tint_<key>`. It is a shared copy of a catalogue base with the base's texture and roughness and the palette colour as `_BaseColor`. The wall base is the texture `building:material` names when it is brick or stone (`brick_red`, `stone_warm`, `stone_light`), else `plaster_white`. The roof base is `roof:material`'s, else `roof_tile` under a pitched shape and `roof_flat_grey` otherwise. The configuration defines every tint of every base present (`_with_tints`), and the GLB carries only the tints in use. So colour adds at most 12 × 4 wall and 7 × 2 roof materials, plus party copies, and in practice a handful. `materials apply` and `dress style` treat a tint as its base: the same photograph in greyscale under the tint, the same repeat, roof UVs for a tinted roof (`material_base`).
8. **Origins.** Everything read from these tags is `observed`. A height worked out from levels is `estimated`. `facade.materials` names the tag that chose each material (`wall_source`, `roof_source`). `detail_report.json` counts `roof_shapes` (`<roof:shape>:<drawn>`) and `building_parts` (kept, height origin, roofs, skipped by issue).

## Consequences

- Kraków's old town now shows its gabled and hipped tenements, its parts and its green copper roofs. See `docs/07-detail.md` for the measured triangles and materials.
- The building outline is still drawn at its own height when it has parts. Under S3DB the outline should give way to its parts (a church whose outline carries the tower's height should not become one block). That needs phase 03 and the collider to follow the parts, and it is left for a later increment.
- A courtyard block keeps a flat roof under a gabled tag. A straight skeleton would be needed to draw it.
- The trim-sheet atlas (in flight on `feat/trim-sheets`) keeps a wall colour without a strip as its own material, so a tint stays a material of its own there. The tint is a colour over a base texture, so a future strip can be baked from the base's grain × the palette colour, with no new photograph. Twelve wall strips do not fit the current atlas layout, so that would be its own decision.
- A colour that is not a CSS name or a hex value (`#rgb` or `#rrggbb`) is ignored, and the material tag or the palette by use stands.
