# ADR-016 — Split OSM block footprints by Catastro building parts

Status: accepted · 30 October 2026.

## Context

In many Spanish city centres OSM maps a whole city block as one `building=*` polygon. Around 40.40657, −3.719187 (Madrid, 150 m radius) the extract has 19 building polygons; 15 are `building=apartments` polygons that each cover a block, 3 have `building:levels` and none has `height`. A real run logged `heights 13 buildings inferred 100%`: every block got one estimated height, one wall texture and one door plan. A one-storey bakery and the five-storey house next to it came out as the same box.

Catastro already describes each construction separately: one INSPIRE `BuildingPart` per volume, with its own `numberOfFloorsAboveGround` and its parcel reference (`refcat`). `cadastre match` reads those parts, but it reduces every part inside an OSM footprint to one overlap-weighted median storey count, so the block stays uniform. And `run` never called the cadastre step, so even that median was not used.

## Decision

1. **A new stage between `geo` and `heights`: `cadastre split`.** It reads the immutable `geo_scene.json` and a downloaded Catastro GML and writes a *new* directory `data/processed/cadastre/` with:
   - `geo_scene.json`: the same contract (`onward_generator.geo_scene` 0.2.0) with some building footprints replaced by sub-footprints, plus a top-level `subdivision` block (input scene hash, Catastro provenance, options, counts);
   - `heights_overrides.catastro.json`: storey overrides for every sub-building and for the matched footprints that were not split (the `cadastre match` rule);
   - `cadastre_match_report.json`: the match report plus a `split` section.
   Every later stage (`heights`, `generate`, `detail`, `dress`, layout, export) reads that scene instead of the `geo` one. They see sub-buildings as ordinary buildings, so heights, wall textures, doors, shopfronts and interiors vary per construction without changes to those stages. `geo` stays free of non-OSM data and the original scene is never rewritten.
2. **What is split.** A footprint is split when it has a Catastro part with at least one storey above ground, all its parts (including those with 0 storeys) cover at least `split_min_coverage` (0.8) of it and, after clipping to the footprint and dropping slivers, at least two *groups* remain. A group is the union of the parts that share a parcel (`refcat`) and a storey count. A sliver is a clipped piece below `min_piece_area_m2` (6 m²) or narrower than `min_piece_width_m` (2 m, an inward buffer of half that width leaves nothing). OSM and Catastro outlines are often offset by tens of centimetres; without the width test a neighbouring parcel would leave a long strip of the wrong height along the façade. Landmarks (the `heights` landmark rules) are not split; ADR-008 handles them as one volume.
3. **Geometry.** Each group is clipped to the footprint and the groups are made disjoint in id order. Area that no group covers (gaps, slivers, misalignment) is assigned piece by piece to the nearest group, ties broken by the longest shared boundary and then the id. Area covered only by parts with 0 storeys above ground (a courtyard over a basement) becomes open ground when it is not a sliver. Coordinates are snapped to 1 mm, like `geo`. Each connected polygon becomes its own sub-building: two pieces of one group that touch only at a corner would give `generate` non-manifold walls (seen on real Madrid data). A stray piece under the minimum area that ends up on its own (it touched its group only at a corner, or not at all) is left as open ground and reported as `dropped_m2`. The sub-footprints, the courtyards and those crumbs together equal the OSM footprint.
4. **Identity and provenance.** A sub-building id is `<osm id>:<smallest localId in its group>`, for example `osm-way-101:0000001AA0000A_part1`; when a group has several polygons, the largest keeps that id and the others get `.2`, `.3`, … in order of decreasing area. It keeps the parent's OSM `source`, `tags`, `clip_status` and the non-height `attributes`. It gets `parent: {"id": <osm id>}` and `subdivision: {source, provider, license, license_url, attribution, retrieved_at, refcat, parts, levels, leftover_m2}`. The parent's observed `height_m` and `levels` describe the whole block, so they move to `subdivision.parent_observed` instead of being applied to every sub-building. This subdivides a building that is in the OSM extract with authoritative data; it never adds a building outside an OSM footprint.
5. **Heights.** Each sub-building gets a `levels` override (`authoritative_levels`, confidence 0.85, or 0.7 when assigned leftover is more than a fifth of its area). `heights_report.json` gets `counts.subdivided`: OSM buildings split, sub-buildings and their storey range.
6. **Party walls and steps.** No change is needed downstream (amended: the parts of one parcel are one building to `detail`, see *One parcel, one building* below). `generate` extrudes every edge of every footprint, so the taller neighbour's side wall above the lower roof is closed. `detail` already marks an edge with another footprint just outside it as a party wall: no doors, windows or shopfronts on shared edges.
7. **POIs.** `detail` matches a shop or amenity point to the footprint that *contains* it; only a point inside no footprint falls back to the 2 m distance rule. A bakery inside a one-storey sub-building gives that sub-building the shopfront, not the five-storey neighbour 1 m away. The fallback takes the nearest footprint only, the lowest id on a tie, for the shopfront and the facade use alike (`docs/07-detail.md`, *One building per POI*).
8. **`run` uses Catastro by default in Spain.** When the map centre is in a territory the DG Catastro WFS serves (`config/catastro_zones.json`: the peninsula, the Balearic and Canary Islands, Ceuta and Melilla, without Navarre and the Basque Country; amended 30 October 2026, see Consequences), `run` adds `catastro` (download, network, retried like Overpass: three times on 429, 5xx and dropped connections, waits doubling from 8 s) and `cadastre` (split) after `geo`. `run --no-catastro` stores `"catastro": false` in `map.json` and keeps the old chain; `--catastro` turns it back on. Outside Spain nothing changes.

## Amendment — robustness on large maps (P11, 30 October 2026)

With Catastro, Madrid (40.40657, −3.719187) reached export at r150 but failed `generate` at r200 (4 critical issues) and r300 (5). Diagnosis:

- **Pinched sub-buildings (r200 and r300, 2 buildings, 4 issues).** After the 1 mm snap, a group's polygon kept a hole touching its outline at one vertex. The polygon is valid, but the extruded walls share that vertical edge between two rings, so `building_mesh_not_manifold` and `collider_not_closed` fired on the same id. Fix: `split` cuts a 2 cm diamond notch at each such contact before sub-buildings are formed (a few cm², added to `dropped_m2`).
- **Crossing slab (r300, 1 issue).** Two crossings met at a corner and left it almost parallel. The 1 cm disk that opens the corner was not snapped: one cut point lay microns from a disk vertex and the 0.1 mm weld merged them. Snapped, the two rims were still under 1 mm apart and touched again. Fix (`generation/mesh.open_point_contacts`): snap the cut and double the disk until the snapped slab has no contact. This is a generation fix, not Catastro's, and is documented in docs/06.
- **Hip roof on a jogged sub-building (r200, `detail`).** Once `generate` passed, `detail` reported `roof_area_mismatch` on a near-convex 18 m² sub-building with a 4 cm step: the hip fan from the centroid folded one face. Fix: the hip stays flat when the centroid does not see every eave (docs/07).

**Quarantine policy.** The phase 03 gate stays all or nothing. A stage that can make an object valid does so where the object is created. Where it cannot, it quarantines that object: `split` checks every sub-building like `generate` checks a building (no open or non-manifold edge above the base). If one fails, that OSM footprint is kept unsplit (status `quarantined`), it gets the `match` override, and the report records `quarantined_m2` and the `reason`. If the quarantined area is above `max_quarantine_share` (5%) of the scene's building area, `split` fails: past that, the problem is the method, not one block. With the fixes above, Madrid r200 and r300 quarantine nothing.

## Amendment — one parcel, one building (setbacks, 30 October 2026)

On the headset, Madrid (40.40657, −3.719187, r150) showed windowless boxes of other colours above the street facades. They were Catastro parts of the *same* parcel with more storeys than the street part: setback storeys (*áticos*, *sobreáticos*) and taller rear wings. Decision 6 assumed that every shared edge is a party wall between neighbours, and `detail` treated every sub-building as its own building. So each part drew its own wall and avoided its touching siblings' walls (a different colour by construction), its estimated roof grammar could put a hip on the terrace under the setback, and the setback's wall over the terrace was a blank party wall.

Decision: `detail` reads the split's parcel as one building identity (`docs/07-detail.md`).

- **Appearance.** The key is `parcel:<refcat>`, the parent's OSM id for a sub-building without one, and the building id otherwise. The first part of a parcel in id order resolves the facade use from the evidence of every part and draws the wall for the key, avoiding the walls around any part; the other parts take the same. The roof grammar and roof material are drawn for the key too. A building outside a split keeps its own id as key, so nothing changes for it.
- **Setback walls.** A party wall whose 1 m probe meets only parts of its own parcel is that building's facade above the highest of their roofs: the template's bays are laid there, storeys below that roof are skipped, and the band the lower part hides is dropped from the visual mesh like a hidden party wall. A wall that meets another parcel stays a blank `party_<wall>` medianera.
- **Terrace.** A part with a taller part of its own parcel within `probe_m` gets no estimated hip; it keeps its flat roof and parapet.

Measured on that Madrid map (9 OSM blocks split into 72 sub-buildings, each block a single parcel): before, all 8 parcels with several parts had parts in different wall materials; after, none. 442 setback walls, 217 with openings (429 openings); most of the rest are shorter than a bay. Visible blank party wall area fell from 10 453 m² to 4 608 m².

## Rejected alternatives

- **Split inside `geo`.** `geo` would mix a second source into the OSM normalisation and need the network.
- **Split inside `heights`.** `heights` writes one record per scene building. Every later stage reads footprints from the scene, so sub-footprints in `heights.json` would be ignored by `generate` and `detail`.
- **Per-part heights on one block footprint (stepped roof only).** One building would still get one texture, one door plan and one shop decision.
- **Replacing the OSM footprint by the Catastro parts.** The outline would change to Catastro's and buildings Catastro has but OSM lacks could appear. The OSM outline stays the envelope.

## Consequences

- More buildings per map: draw calls and triangles rise with the number of sub-buildings. Shared walls add hidden faces back to back.
- The first area test was a CRS and a latitude band. Superseded on 30 October 2026: coverage is a validated table of territory polygons with the request CRS per UTM zone (EPSG:25829, 25830 and 25831 on the peninsula, 25831 in the Balearics, 32628 and 32627 in the Canaries: the CRS the service lists in its GetCapabilities). Navarre and the Basque Country are excluded. Parts are converted from the request CRS to the map CRS with pyproj. `docs/05-cadastre.md#coverage`.
- A map larger than about 900 m across used to keep the old chain. Superseded on 30 October 2026: a larger box is downloaded in tiles of at most 1 km², merged with the parts repeated across tile edges dropped by `localId`, every tile recorded in the sidecar. Up to 25 tiles.
- Catastro geometry now shapes the exported meshes, not only a number. That is a transformation the Catastro licence allows; the attribution is carried as before (`docs/legal/data-sources.md`).
