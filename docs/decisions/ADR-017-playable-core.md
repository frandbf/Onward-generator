# ADR-017 — A playable core with full detail and a scenic ring outside it

Status: accepted · 30 October 2026. Plan items P16, P17 and P18 (`docs/plans/2026-10-30-scale-and-speed.md`), first increment.

## Context

The Quest triangle budget is now what limits the size of a map, not the geometry or the time it takes to build:

- The guide gives about 250 000 triangles and about 150 draw calls per view, players, objectives and SDK prefabs included (ADR-005). The Murcia measurement already sat at 247 627 triangles in Unity.
- With Catastro, Madrid at a 300 m radius reaches export with 510 861 visual triangles from `detail`, twice the guide. At 200 m it has 217 845, before props, signs and SDK prefabs.
- Every building gets the same treatment wherever it stands: openings, balconies, shopfronts, interiors, signs, a collider. A building 250 m from the centre costs as much as the one the player stands beside, and nobody can reach it.
- Fixed limits (16 generated benches, 10 bins, 6 manhole covers, the overgrow caps, outdoor light probes on the navmesh) are spread over the whole site, so their density falls about 27 times from r100 to r500.
- Navigation spacing is derived from the walkable area and a 320-node heuristic (`docs/11-onward-sdk.md`): `sqrt(area / 320)`. Above r≈150 it is over 15 m, 34.7 m at r300.

ADR-014 hides the edge of the area with fog and an optional backdrop quad ring, and names the next step: real building silhouettes, without openings, no collision, no lightmap, few renderers.

## Decision

1. **A playable core.** `map.json` gets `core_radius_m`: the half-side of a square around the map centre (the same convention as `radius_m`), in local metres. `map create --core-radius-m` and `run --core-radius-m` set it, default 150 m (since 5 November 2026 `min(150, radius − 50)`; amendment below). `0`, a value at or above `radius_m`, or a map written before the key existed (absent) means no core: the whole map is played and every stage behaves as before. Otherwise it must be at least 50 m.
2. **One tier per building.** `detail --core-radius-m` (the `next:` line and `run` read it from `map.json`) tiers each building: `core` when its footprint meets the open core square, `scenic` otherwise. A `building_part` and a landmark tower take the tier of their building. The tier is stored on every building record (`tier`), and `scene_detail.json` gets `core: {half_side_m, buildings: {core, scenic}}` (`null` without a core). Later stages read the core from that block, never from `map.json`.
3. **Inside the core, nothing changes.** Today's pipeline: facades, openings, doors, interiors, shops, signs, urban elements, overgrow, collision, navigation.
4. **The scenic tier is a plain shell.** A scenic building is its OSM (or Catastro) footprint extruded to its height (ADR-016 heights): walls and a flat roof cap, no base. No openings, doors, balconies, parapets, hip roofs, towers, interiors or shops (so `signs` draws no shop sign or vinyl for it). No collider. Walls use one of three shared materials, `scenic_wall_light`, `scenic_wall_warm` and `scenic_wall_ochre`, picked by `sha256(seed, building, "scenic")`; every roof is `roof_flat_grey`. `signs` puts no street plate outside the core either: plates are for the player, and when this was decided each plate was its own material, so a plate in the ring was a draw of its own (since 3 November 2026 the plates share atlas pages). `signs` reads the scene `detail` built from (the Catastro split where it ran, since 4 November 2026; before, the OSM scene), and it keeps a wall by the position of its midpoint, not by building id. `materials apply` gives the three scenic walls a plaster photograph in greyscale (the `SHARED_FACADE_SLOTS` rule), so the tint stays on `_BaseColor`. Windows painted into the texture were left out: it needs a second image per tone and a UV rule per storey, and the ring is seen from at least the width of a street, behind fog.
5. **No props or collision outside the core.** `detail` drops urban elements whose position is outside the core and counts them (`counts.tiers.scenic.urban_elements_dropped`). `dress style` places its generated benches and bins on free ground inside the core only. `overgrow place` clips its zone to the core. The ground colliders (`collider/walkable`, `collider/carriageway`, `collider/walkable_crossing`) are rebuilt as closed slabs over the part of their polygon inside the core. The visual ground, curbs and markings stay over the whole site: they are seen, not walked.
6. **The wall moves to the core edge.** `bounds` puts the 12 m perimeter collider on the core square (intersected with the site) when the manifest has a core. Players cannot reach the ring.
7. **Navigation only in the core (P18).** The adapter bakes the navmesh from the walkable colliders, so with item 5 the baked area is the core. `perf layout --playable` intersects its free ground and navmesh islands with the core inside the wall, so spawns, Uplink, Hunt routes, ammo, Escort, Evac and hard drives stay in the core. An ammo crate (`AmmoBox.prefab`) stays 3 m (`AMMO_SPAWN_CLEAR_M`) from every point of the four spawn blocks: the first crate went to the pole of the free ground, where the attackers' block already stood, and on 20 of 21 maps a crate was 0.5 to 0.8 m from a spawn point (madridescaleras2 0.71 m, now 30.67 m). The Escort, Evac and hard-drive points in turn stay 3 m (`AMMO_OBJECTIVE_CLEAR_M`) from every crate: they were scattered from the same pole, so on Barcelona Sarria (5 November 2026) both Escort points stood exactly on a crate and a hard drive 0.75 m from one. `layout.json` gets `navigation: {walkable_area_m2, span_m, node_spacing_estimate_m}` from the adapter's rule `max(1, span / 400, sqrt(area / 320))`. It is an estimate; the adapter measures the baked mesh.
8. **Limits apply to the core (P17).** The fixed limits stay absolute and are not scaled with area. They are spent inside the core, so a map with a 150 m core has the prop and probe density of a 150 m map whatever its radius. The outdoor light probes follow the navmesh bounds in the adapter, so they follow the core with no C# change.
9. **Lightmap.** `export.lightmap` gives the class `scenic` (materials starting `scenic_`) a scale of 0.25, the roof scale. The scenic roof is a roof material (0.25). Not baked at all (probe lit) was rejected for this increment: it needs `ContributesGi` in the adapter to change, and that C# cannot be compiled here.
10. **Fog starts at the wall.** `map create` writes `fog_start_m` = `core_radius_m` when there is a core (radius + margin otherwise), so the ring fades into the fog.
11. **Reports.** `detail_report.json` gets `counts.tiers`: per tier the buildings, visual and collision triangles. `onward_export.json` gets `tiers`: per tier `core`, `scenic` and `shared` the visual triangles and the predicted draws after the adapter's (cell, material) combine, plus the total predicted draws. A node goes to the tier of its building record; a node with no tier (ground, curbs, markings, props, the backdrop) goes to `core` when its bounds lie inside the core, `scenic` when they lie outside, and `shared` when they cross the edge. `run` prints the tier triangles on the `detail` line.

## Draw calls

The adapter combines visual meshes by (100 m cell, material) (ADR-005). A ring cell holds at most three scenic walls and one roof material, against the ten or so facade materials, glass, frames, doors, signs and shop materials a core cell holds. So a ring cell costs about 4 draws, plus the ground pieces whose centre falls in it. With the core at 150 m, an r300 map has about 35 ring cells; frustum culling leaves part of them in view. Measured on Madrid r300 (`docs/09-perf.md#playable-core`): the ring adds 140 uncullled draws and the core about 260, against 827 for the whole map without a core. These are predictions from the GLB, not a headset measurement.

## Relation to ADR-014

The scenic ring is the step ADR-014 left open, done inside the area already extracted instead of in a band outside it: the buildings are the OSM footprints of the map, with no extra download and no new licence. The fog now starts at the core wall. The backdrop quad ring stays optional at the site edge, beyond the scenic ring, to close the horizon behind the last buildings.

## Rejected alternatives

- **A triangle-budget mode** (pick the core from a triangle target). Rejected for the first increment: the cost of a core is only known after `detail` runs, so it needs a search over several `detail` runs or a model of the cost per building. Since 7 November 2026 it is an opt-in mode with a cost model from one run; see the amendment below.
- **A disc core.** The map, the Catastro tiles and the adapter cells are squares, and a square core keeps the wall to four straight sides.
- **Keeping each scenic building's own wall material.** About ten wall materials per ring cell, each its own draw, and the scenic lightmap scale cannot be told apart from the core one when a ring building and a core building share a material in a cell.
- **Tier-aware combining or lighting in the adapter.** Needs C# that cannot be compiled or run here.

## Consequences

- Inside the core, buildings get today's treatment. The interior quota and the triangle budgets are now spent on core buildings only, so the core can get interiors the ring used to take. A map without a core does not change, apart from `core: null` in `scene_detail.json`, `tiers: null` in `onward_export.json` and the `navigation` estimate in `layout.json`.
- The ring has no colliders and no navmesh: a bot or a player cannot leave the core, and the wall is the edge.
- A landmark in the ring is a plain box (`needs_custom_model` stays on its record).
- Must be checked on the headset: draws in the two fixed views (A and B), the frame time on the route, how the ring reads through the fog at the wall, the bake time and pages, and the light on the ring buildings at scale 0.25.

## Amendment, 5 November 2026: a default core keeps a 50 m scenic ring

The default core was 150 m whatever the radius, so a 150 m map had no core and the whole map was played. Barcelona Sarria (r 150) then reached 380 811 triangles in Unity, 12 lightmap pages and 135 / 318 / 376 draws a view (median, p90, max), against about 250 000 triangles and 150 draws in the guide. Without `--core-radius-m` the core is now `min(150, radius − 50)` (`default_core`, `CORE_RING_DEFAULT_M` 50); under the 50 m minimum, so under a 100 m radius, the whole map is played. A map of 200 m or more keeps its 150 m core. The same Barcelona map with a 100 m core: 234 166 triangles, 5 lightmap pages, 90 / 184 / 216 draws a view, and no lift, since no building of 3 to 6 storeys in that core had room for a cabin. An explicit `--core-radius-m`, 0 included, is kept as before, and a `map.json` already written keeps its value.

## Amendment, 7 November 2026: budget mode

Status: proposed until a headset session checks it (plan B1, `docs/plans/2026-11-07-workshop-quality.md`). The fixed radius stays the default.

The guide in the workshop plan is at most 220 000 triangles in Unity. With the fixed 150 m core, Madrid r300 exports 270 361 core triangles, so the core has to be chosen by what it costs, not by its size.

1. **Opt-in.** `map create --core-target-triangles N` and `run --core-target-triangles N` on a new map write `map.json` `core: {mode: "budget", target_triangles: N, max_half_side_m: M}`, N from 1000 to 2 000 000. M is `--core-radius-m` when given, else `radius_m − 50`, so the 50 m ring stays; it must lie between 50 and `radius_m − 50`, and a map without a playable core cannot have one. `core_radius_m` and the fog are written as before. Without the key nothing changes. On an existing map, `run` refuses a target that `map.json` does not already record: the target is set at map create. The `detail` `next:` line, and so `run`, pass `--core-radius-m M --core-target-triangles N`. Another mode in `map.json` (such as `polygon`) is refused when it is read.
2. **Shape: the square stays.** Only its half-side is solved. `core.half_side_m` in `scene_detail.json` becomes an output, with the same key and meaning, so `tiers.core_of` and its consumers (the `bounds` wall, `dress`, `overgrow`, `signs`, `perf layout` and `perf climbs`, the export tiers and `view_draws`) and the adapter's wall and navmesh do not change. A polygon core would change that contract across eight stages and the C# adapter; it needs its own decision and must not reuse the `core` keys silently.
3. **Seed: the map centre.** The plan said to grow the core from the spawns and the routes, but `perf layout` places them after `detail` and `bounds`. A route-aware seed is a later increment.
4. **Cost model, one run.** `detail` runs once with the core at M (the probe). A building's cost is the visual triangles of its core objects there: the building, its `building:part` volumes and its landmark towers, so a building is taken whole and never split. Its interior is costed apart, in the stair or the shop pool, each capped at its triangle budget (`interiors.max_triangles`, `interiors.shops.max_triangles`), and counts only when the core covers the whole footprint, the rule `detail` applies. An interior the probe refused for that budget is costed at the triangles its refusal names. The first model, one constant interior reserve, missed the synthetic r150 grid by 33 %: the buildings across the edge lose their interiors.
5. **Solve, no search.** Each footprint has a reach (its L∞ distance from the centre; it is core when the reach is under the half-side) and an extent (its largest `max(|x|, |y|)`; its interior counts when the extent is at most the half-side). `tiers.solve_core` tries 50 m, M and the midpoint between every two neighbouring reaches or extents, keeps the prediction nearest N, and the smaller core on a tie; under a target the largest core cannot reach it keeps M. `detail` then runs once at that half-side, unless it is M and the probe is kept. Two runs at most.
6. **The figure.** The target and the ±5 % check are `counts.tiers.core.visual_triangles` of the kept run, not the model and not the Unity total. `scene_detail.json` `core` adds `mode`, `target_triangles`, `max_half_side_m`, `predicted_triangles`, `achieved_triangles`, `tolerance` and `within_tolerance`. `core_budget_floor` and `core_budget_ceiling` (info) say the target is under the 50 m core or over the core at M; `core_budget_missed` (warning) says the kept run is more than 5 % off for another reason. None is critical.
7. **Not additive.** Party walls are hidden only between core shells, so a building next to one that turns scenic shows more wall; the interior queue refills in its sha256 order and the shops by distance. That is why the check reads the kept run. The tolerance holds when N is much larger than one building: the square grows a building at a time, and a map of a few large buildings can miss it (`core_budget_missed`).
8. **What N does not count.** Dress, overgrow, signs, the ground and the SDK prefabs come on top in Unity: on the bench, the exported core is 1.68 times the `detail` core in Murcia r150, 1.51 times in Madrid r300 and 1.44 times in Madrid r500 (`docs/09-perf.md#budget-core`), and the scenic ring comes on top. To aim at 220 000 in Unity, N is that figure over the ratio, less the ring; the ratio is a measurement, not a rule.
9. **Fog.** `export.json` `fog_start_m` is written by `map create` from `core_radius_m`, before the core is solved, so with a budget core the fog may start before or past the wall. Not changed here (export scope); the headset session checks it.

Measured with synthetic grids (r150, r300, r500) in `tests/integration/test_core_budget.py` and on the bench inputs in `docs/09-perf.md#budget-core`. To check on the headset before the status is accepted: Unity `scene_report.json` triangles against N, draws in the fixed views A and B (at most 150), the median fps on the route (at least 72), and the ring through the fog at the moved wall.

## Amendment, 8 November 2026: scenic walls out of the lightmap (decision 9)

Status: proposed until a Unity bake and a headset session check it (plan C4, `docs/plans/2026-11-07-workshop-quality.md`). Off by default; decision 9 stays the default.

Decision 9 rejected a probe-lit ring because the adapter's `ContributesGi` would have to change. The change is now made, behind an option:

1. **Opt-in.** The export card's `build.scenic_out_of_lightmap` (boolean, absent means off) reaches `adapter_options.json` only when the card sets it. A card without the key gives the same package, `package_id` included, and an older package bakes the ring as before (the adapter's `Options` default is `false`).
2. **Walls only.** With the option on, a material whose name starts `scenic_` (`M_scenic_*` in Unity: the three scenic walls and `scenic_trim_<style>`) takes no lightmap texels (`scaleInLightmap = 0`) and `receiveGI = LightProbes`; combined, it takes the ambient light (`lightProbeUsage = Off`), as trees and props do (`docs/12-export.md`, *Out of the lightmap*). The scenic roofs share `roof_flat_grey` with the core's flat roofs and stay baked at 0.25. Covering them needs a scenic roof material of its own or tier-aware grouping, both of which change the draws; that is a later increment.
3. **Still a shadow caster.** The walls keep `ContributeGI`, so the baked sun still casts their shadows on the core, the reason props stopped leaving GI on 4 November 2026.
4. **Mirror.** `export.lightmap.contributes_gi`, `material_scales` and `estimate` take `scenic_out_of_lightmap`; `lightmap_estimate` then has no `scenic` class. A test pins the C# prefix `m_scenic_` to `"m_" + tiers.SCENIC_PREFIX`.

On the bench (`docs/09-perf.md#scenic-walls-out-of-the-lightmap`) the estimate falls by the scenic class: Madrid r300 −11.46 M texels (27 %, 11 → 8 pages), Murcia r150 −1.88 M (14 %, 4 → 3), Kraków −0.78 M (11 %, 2 pages), Paris 0 (no core). Triangles and draws do not change. These are estimates, not a bake.

Risks, all for the bake and the headset: ADR-014 measured a renderer lit by the ambient light alone at about 48 % of its albedo (its backdrop then moved to the unlit graph), so the ring may read slate-dark against the fog; a brightness step may show where a lightmapped core facade meets an ambient-lit scenic wall in a shared cell; the lit roofs over ambient walls may disagree from above; without combining (`combine_cell_m` 0) each scenic renderer samples probes from outside the core's probe hull. The bake seconds may not fall in step with the texels: the walls still cast shadows in the bake and are still unwrapped.

To check before the status is accepted: the C# compiles in Unity 2021.3.23f1; the scene log's `scaleInLightmap (scale x renderers)` line shows the scenic renderers at 0 with the option and at 0.25 without; `task unity -- --bake draft` and `--bake final` with the option off and on, compared with `tools/unity/report_timings.py`; and on Quest 3 the ring's brightness against the fog, the seam at the core wall, at least 72 fps median and at most 150 draws in views A and B.

## Amendment, 9 November 2026: an intermediate HLOD ring (plan D3)

Status: proposed until a Unity bake and a headset session check it (plan D3, `docs/plans/2026-11-07-workshop-quality.md`). Off by default; the two tiers stay the default.

ADR-014's next step was silhouettes past the core, and plan 2026-10-31 proposed boxes with an atlas cell at 50 to 150 triangles. Between a detailed core building (about 1 180 triangles) and a scenic box (44) there was nothing.

1. **Opt-in.** `map.json` `"mid_ring_m"` (metres, 10 to 1000; absent is off), `detail --mid-ring-m` and `bench.py run --mid-ring-m`. It needs a core, fixed or budget: `MapError` or `CoreError` otherwise, before anything is written. `run` passes the flag after the core flags, and a changed, added or removed width makes `detail` stale. Without the key every output and the bench are byte-identical.
2. **Band rule.** `tiers.ring_tier`: the core is decided first with `building_tier`, unchanged. A building that is not core and shares area with the square of half-side `core.half_side_m + mid_ring_m` is `mid`; one that only touches its outer edge stays `scenic`. Buildings are taken whole, and `building:part` volumes take their building's tier and lose their collider outside the core. The half-side is the core `detail` used: with a budget core the ring follows the solved half-side, never `map.json`. `building_costs` counts only `core` records, so the budget solve and its N do not change.
3. **Mid shell** (`detail/mid.py`). The footprint extruded to its height in its scenic wall (`scenic_wall`, as in decision 4), the roof form of its roof plan (an OSM `roof:shape`, the estimated or the template hip, the nDSM gable, or a flat cap without a parapet; gables in the wall material), and painted windows: ADR-025 flat openings, a hole and one `glass_dark` quad in fixed 3 m bays (1.2 × 1.4 m, 0.9 m sill) on the walls `refine_exposure` calls exposed. Rows (one wall, one storey) are added whole, longest wall first, then the lower storey, then the wall index, while the building stays at or under 150 triangles; one that does not fit is skipped. A pitched roof that would take walls and roof over 150 is drawn flat (`roof.fallback: mid_triangle_cap`). No doors, balconies, parapets, towers, interiors, shops, signs or colliders (`collider.source: none_mid`). A landmark draws no windows. Street elements outside the core are still dropped (section 5).
4. **Materials and lightmap.** No new material and no atlas change: the trim maps every atlas part of a node whose record is `mid`, its windows included, to `scenic_trim_<style>` (ADR-020 decision 4), so the walls keep the scenic lightmap class (0.25) and the C4 option covers them. Roofs keep their roof materials, which may add (cell, material) groups in ring cells; on the legacy path `glass_dark` stays its own material.
5. **Contract.** Only when on: `scene_detail.json` `core` gains `mid_ring_m` and `buildings.mid`; records may have `tier: mid`, `facade.template` and `representation` `mid_shell`, `facade.mid {windows, exposed_faces, cap}`, `facade.storeys` and `checks.facade_open_edges`; `detail_report.json` `counts.tiers.mid` has `buildings`, `visual_triangles`, `collision_triangles` and `triangles_per_building {mean, min, max}`; `onward_export.json` `tiers.mid` has `visual_triangles` and `draws`. Provenance: the shell and its windows are `attribute_origin: generated`; `roof.origin` is the roof plan's (observed tag, estimated hip or nDSM, generated).
6. **Rejected.** A Unity `LODGroup` (ADR-025: the SDK and the combine would merge the levels), dynamic LOD (no runtime code in the map), a windowed wall strip in the atlas (it would change the atlas bytes and `TRIM_BAKE_VERSION`), and window quads laid over the wall (z-fighting at distance).

On the bench with a 100 m ring ([09-perf.md](../09-perf.md#intermediate-hlod-ring)) the core does not change; triangles rise 150 339 → 170 603 (Murcia), 398 025 → 460 922 (Madrid, already over the 220 000 target), 72 884 → 79 940 (Kraków), Paris has no core; view draws p90 rise by 5 to 17. Not a headset measurement. Cross-references: ADR-014 (next step), ADR-020 (scenic shells on the trim atlas), ADR-023 (roof shapes), ADR-025 (flat openings, no LODGroup).

To check before the status is accepted: the checklist in [09-perf.md](../09-perf.md#intermediate-hlod-ring).
