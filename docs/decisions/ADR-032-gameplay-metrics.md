# ADR-032 — Gameplay metrics, as a report only

Status: proposed · 7 November 2026 (plan [2026-11-07-workshop-quality](../plans/2026-11-07-workshop-quality.md), increment B4). It stays proposed until the playtest of plan 4.2 ("Partida de prueba con jugadores al cerrar B4 y C6") has compared the reported numbers with what players feel. Only then can a threshold become a check that blocks anything.

Cites [ADR-017](ADR-017-playable-core.md) (the metrics are computed inside the playable core and its wall), [ADR-015](ADR-015-walkable-crossings.md) (the walkable crossings join the navmesh), [ADR-005](ADR-005-quest-performance.md) (the budget: a report costs nothing at runtime) and [ADR-029](ADR-029-ladders-and-lifts.md) (ladders and lifts are not on the navmesh: the AI does not climb). It amends none of them.

## Context

Plan §1 names four "Juego" criteria: at least 3 distinct routes between the spawns with no pair overlapping more than 50 %, the longest sightline without cover along the routes at most 60 m, a difference of at most 15 % between the teams' times to first contact, and N cover colliders of at least 0.5 m per 100 m of route. Nothing measured them. `perf layout --playable` places the spawn blocks, the Uplink and the Hunt routes, but its routes are bot patrols along the sidewalks, not the routes between the spawns, and the module says it is "a measurement fixture, not a gameplay design". C6 (spawns placed by these metrics) and C7 (cover where they show long open sightlines) depend on the numbers; this increment only measures.

## Decision

1. **A report, off by default.** `perf layout --playable --gameplay-report` also writes `gameplay_report.json` beside `layout.json` (schema `onward_generator.gameplay_report` 0.1.0). `run` passes the flag when `map.json` has `"gameplay_report": true` (a missing key is off; a value that is not a bool is refused). `tools/bench/bench.py run --gameplay-report` sets that key in the run copies only, so the frozen inputs and `inputs_sha256` do not change. The layout document, `layout.json` bytes, the export package (`PAYLOAD`, `_check_layout`) and the GLBs are unchanged with the option on or off; the package never carries the report.
2. **Estimated, never blocking.** The report has `provenance: estimated` and a note that the navmesh is a 2D approximation; the adapter's baked navmesh in Unity is the real one (`docs/11-onward-sdk.md`). `proposed_checks` gives `pass`, `fail` or `null` for each proposed threshold and `blocking: false`. No check changes an exit code or stops an export. A missing spawn block (`spawn_missing`), a spawn or the Uplink farther than 2 m from the graph (`spawn_off_navmesh`, `uplink_off_navmesh`) or no path (`no_path`) is a status with null metrics, not an exception. A collision GLB without a cover collider is `no_cover`, and the other metrics are still measured. An unreadable collision GLB, or a layout that is not `gameplay_layout` 0.3.0, exits 1 and writes neither file.
3. **The route graph.** Nodes every `GRID_M` = 2 m, aligned to `floor(bounds / 2 m)`, inside `perf.layout.nav_islands(base, playable_area(manifest))` (free ground and the crossings, clipped to the core, shrunk by the 0.35 m agent radius), reused as is. Furniture does not cut the graph, as it does not cut the baked navmesh; ladders and lifts are not on it. Eight neighbours: an edge is kept when the whole segment lies on the islands, and a diagonal also needs both orthogonal neighbours. Costs are integer millimetres (2 000 straight, 2 828 diagonal). Dijkstra with `heapq`, ties broken on (cost, node index). A spawn block centre or the Uplink snaps to the nearest node within `SNAP_M` = 2 m, inclusive.
4. **Distinct routes.** Pairs: `attackers` to `defenders` and `attackers_b` to `defenders_b`. Up to `MAX_ROUTES` = 6 attempts per pair, each the shortest path under the current costs. After each attempt every edge whose midpoint lies within `CORRIDOR_M` = 8 m of that path costs `ROUTE_PENALTY` = 3 times more. A candidate is kept when its overlap with every kept route is at most `MAX_OVERLAP` = 0.5. **Overlap**(a, b): the cost of the edges of the shorter route whose midpoint lies within 8 m of the other route, divided by the shorter route's length; it is reported for every kept pair. The corridor makes two lanes of one street one route: with shared grid edges only, the first measurement found six "distinct" routes on bench_murcia that were parallel lanes 2 m apart in the same streets.
5. **Colliders.** The collision GLB of the playable directory, read with `generation.gltf.nodes_from_glb` (node matrices applied, glTF y-up and −z north mapped to local x east, y north). Each node becomes its axis-aligned box. Left out: `collider/walkable`, `collider/carriageway`, `collider/walkable_crossing` and any `ground_collider`; `building/`, `building_part/` and `interior/` (buildings come from `base.partition.buildings`, the observed OSM footprints); any node whose bottom is above `GROUND_CONTACT_M` = 0.5 m (a tower part on a roof). The perimeter wall (`envelope/`) blocks sight and is never cover. **Cover**: the overgrow rule (`overgrow.ambient.COVER_HEIGHT_M` and `COVER_DEPTH_M`, imported, not copied): at least 0.5 m tall and 0.4 m on the short side.
6. **Longest open sightline.** Route samples every `SAMPLE_STEP_M` = 5 m and at the end. A sample is covered when a cover box lies within `COVER_REACH_M` = 1.5 m. From each uncovered sample, `RAY_COUNT` = 72 rays (every 5°) against the outlines of the building footprints, the boxes of props at least `SIGHT_BLOCK_HEIGHT_M` = 1.2 m tall and the wall's box, capped at `RAY_CAP_M` = 300 m. Rays are queried in 10 m pieces through an `STRtree`; the hit distance is exact. The report keeps the longest ray and both ends in metres. Water and the carriageway do not stop a ray.
7. **Cover density.** Each route is cut into `STRETCH_M` = 25 m stretches (the last one is the remainder). A stretch reports its distinct cover colliders within 1.5 m, `cover_per_100m` = count × 100 / length, and the longest open sightline among its samples. A collider beside two stretches counts in both. N is reported, never judged (`result: null`), until the first measurement and the playtest fix it.
8. **Balance.** The navmesh length from each team's nearer block (main or `_b`) to the Uplink, divided by `RUN_SPEED_M_S` = 4.0 m/s (estimated). `diff_pct` = |a − b| / max(a, b) × 100, rounded to 0.01. The Uplink is the contact point for every mode; that is an assumption.
9. **Determinism.** No randomness, integer costs, index tie-breaks, sorted collider lists and rounded floats; the report is written with sorted keys and carries `layout_sha256`, the SHA-256 of the `layout.json` it was computed from. `run`'s layout line shows the headline only when that hash matches, so a report left by an earlier run is not shown.
10. **Dependencies.** shapely, numpy and `heapq` only. No graph library, no mesh ray caster.

## Consequences

- C6 and C7 can read `gameplay_report.json`; this increment does not act on it. No geometry, spawn, objective or route changes. C6 now acts on the same measures behind an option that is off by default (see the C6 amendment below).
- The 2D grid can differ from the baked navmesh: the baked mesh follows the collision geometry, steps and slopes; the grid follows the partition. Axis-aligned boxes over-estimate a rotated collider. Both are said in the report's note.
- The thresholds, the corridor, the speed, the 1.2 m blocking height, the 1.5 m reach and the Uplink as the contact point are all uncalibrated.
- With the option on, the layout stage takes the report's time too (see the measurements). With it off, nothing runs.

## Headset and playtest checks (pending, not done)

1. Playtest (plan 4.2): two or three real matches on a bench map, Hunt and Uplink, comparing the reported routes, longest open sightlines and first-contact balance with what players feel. It calibrates the §1 thresholds and the cover density N.
2. Optional headset check on a `bench_murcia` package: stand at the four spawn blocks and at both ends of the longest reported sightline, and confirm that the report's open stretches are open ground in the game and that the 2D graph matches the baked navmesh there.
3. After the playtest, move this ADR to accepted or correct it. Only then consider making a metric a blocking gate. C6 already acts on the metrics, off by default, because a playtest does not block code (maintainer note, 8 November 2026); turning it on by default waits for the playtest.

## C6 amendment (proposed) — spawns and the Uplink placed by these metrics

Status: proposed · 8 November 2026 (plan increment C6). It stays proposed with the rest of this ADR until the playtest of plan 4.2, which closes B4 and C6. The playtest does not block the code, so C6 ships behind an option that is off by default.

**Option.** `perf layout --playable --balanced-spawns` (refused without `--playable`, exit 1), `map.json` `"balanced_spawns": true` (missing = off; any value that is not true or false is refused before a stage runs), `bench.py run --balanced-spawns` (run copies only). Off, no new code runs: `layout.json`, `gameplay_report.json` and every package file but `onward_export.json` are byte-identical to HEAD.

**Candidates** (`perf.layout.choose_placement`, fixed order, no randomness, at most 8). The attackers stay at the pole of inaccessibility of the largest free area with 1.5 m clearance. Up to `K_DEFENDERS` = 4 defenders' positions come from `_defender_candidates`: the grid of `_defenders`, farthest first, then north, then east, keeping blocks that fit, lie at least 30 m (`TEAM_SEPARATION_M`) from the attackers and at least 10 m (`BLOCK_GAP_M`) from each other; the first is today's. Each gets two Uplinks: (a) today's midpoint moved onto the area, then (b) the even-contact node: on the shortest attackers-to-defenders path of the navigation graph, the node inside the 1.5 m spawn area where the two teams' distances (each the shorter of its two blocks) differ least in integer millimetres, the lower node index on a tie. The spawn area is one connected piece inside one navmesh island, so the Uplink stays on the island that holds today's, inside the core, and stays the navigation origin. The second blocks come from `extra_spawn_block` exactly as `playable_layout` places them; when they do not fit, the candidate is invalid. Candidate 0 is today's placement.

**Scores** (`gameplay.placement`, the report's own definitions: `BLOCKS`, `PAIRS`, `TEAMS`, `snap` within 2 m, `dijkstra`, `distinct_routes`, `overlap`, `balance`, on the same `nav_graph(nav_islands(...))`). Stage 1, every candidate: `contact_diff_pct` from one Dijkstra from the Uplink. Stage 2, candidate 0 first, then the candidates whose difference is not above candidate 0's, lowest first: `distinct_routes` (the poorer pair) and the largest pairwise overlap. Once one is admissible, higher differences cannot win and are not scored. Routes are cached per node pair, so two Uplinks for one defenders' position share them. A centre or the Uplink more than 2 m from every node, or no path, makes a candidate invalid.

**Choice.** A candidate is admissible when it is valid, has at least candidate 0's routes, at most its contact difference, and an overlap at most max(candidate 0's, 50 %). The lowest (contact difference, −routes, overlap, index) wins; it replaces today's placement only when that key is strictly lower. Otherwise, or when candidate 0 cannot be scored, or when the chosen placement cannot be built (Escort, Evac, drives or climbs), the current placement is kept and `perf layout` says why on its `placement:` line. It never raises where the option-off layout succeeds; the option-off errors are raised as before. The placement is checked again when it is built: same attackers, defenders' block inside the area and 30 m away, Uplink on the area, second blocks inside the Uplink's ground and 10 m from the other centres.

**Output.** No new key: `layout.json` stays `gameplay_layout` 0.3.0 and only the values of `defenders`, `defenders_b`, `attackers_b`, `uplink_m` and what follows from them (routes, AI, ammo, Escort, Evac, drives, climbs, birds) move. `gameplay_report.json`, the export package (`_check_layout`) and the C# adapter are unchanged. One stdout line, `placement: balanced, candidate K of N, contact diff A -> B %, routes R -> S, overlap X -> Y % (estimated from the 2D navmesh)` or `placement: kept the current placement: <reason> (estimated from the 2D navmesh)`. It is not written to any file.

**Alleys and courtyards.** The plan row also asks for "more routes through alleys and courtyards" (`todo.md`). C6 only prefers placements with at least as many distinct routes over the walkable ground the graph already has: pedestrian ways, courtyards in the partition and the ADR-015 crossings. It adds no geometry and no building, and leaves the Hunt patrols as they are, so that item stays partly open.

**Measurements** (Docker on an Apple M4 Pro, frozen bench inputs, `--offline --gameplay-report`, runs `head_off` at workshop/c5, `c6_off`, `c6_on` and `c6_on2`; not a device or playtest measurement). `c6_off` against `head_off`: `bench.py check` finds no difference outside timings, and `layout.json`, `gameplay_report.json` and every package file but `onward_export.json` are byte-identical. `c6_on` against `c6_on2`: no difference outside timings, and `layout.json` and `gameplay_report.json` byte-identical. Against `c6_off`, triangles, draws and pages do not change; the package differs in `layout.json` and in the package id (`adapter_options.json`, `quest_acceptance.md`, `onward_export.json`).

## C6 amendment, 11 November 2026 (proposed) — both teams move, and routes come first within 15 %

Plan Q1 ([2026-11-11-generation-quality](../plans/2026-11-11-generation-quality.md)). On Rue Mouffetard the report gave one distinct route. All eight candidates of the rule above put the defenders at the far end of the attackers' own street, because the attackers never moved and `_defender_candidates` takes the farthest grid points first, 10 m apart. The choice also put the contact difference before the routes, so a candidate with more routes lost to one a little better balanced.

**Candidates.** After those of the rule above, every pair of `_spread_sites` at least 30 m apart, attackers at the first of the pair, each with the same two Uplinks. `_spread_sites` starts at today's attackers and adds, up to `K_SITES` = 6 in all, the grid point whose block fits farthest from the sites already kept (ties to the north, then east), while that distance is at least `SITE_GAP_M` = 20 m. That gives at most 15 more pairs, 38 candidates in all. A placement may now move the attackers: `_check_placement` asks that both blocks fit the spawn area and stand 30 m apart, and `gameplay_layout` takes the attackers, and the map quad with them, from the placement.

**Choice.** Admissibility does not change: valid, at least candidate 0's routes, at most its contact difference, an overlap at most max(candidate 0's, 50 %). Among admissible candidates within `CONTACT_TOLERANCE_PCT` = 15 % (this ADR's proposed balance threshold) the most distinct routes win, then the lower difference, overlap and index. Past 15 % the order of the rule above stands: the difference first. Stage 2 now scores every candidate no less balanced than candidate 0, because within the tolerance a less balanced one with more routes can win.

**A warning.** When the report of the layout exists and its headline has exactly 1 distinct route, `run` warns after the layout stage: `1 distinct route between the spawns: the map plays as a corridor (ADR-032)`. 0 is the report's own `no path` status, not this warning.

## C7 amendment (proposed) — cover on the long open sightlines

Status: proposed · 8 November 2026 (plan increment C7). It stays proposed with the rest of this ADR until the playtest of plan 4.2 and the headset session below. The playtest does not block the code, so C7 ships behind an option that is off by default. Cites [ADR-010](ADR-010-overgrow-ai-plan.md) (code, not a model, reserves the passage; AI stays optional: C7 is rules only), [ADR-017](ADR-017-playable-core.md) (cover stands inside the playable core, never on the wall), [ADR-031](ADR-031-shop-furnishing.md) (1.2 m walk width and the connectivity test by erosion, reused here on the street) and [ADR-005](ADR-005-quest-performance.md) (the triangle and draw budget). It amends none of them.

**Option and stage.** `map.json` `"sightline_cover": true` (missing = off; a value that is not true or false is refused before a stage runs) adds one stage, `cover` (`onward-generator perf cover`), between `layout` and `export`. The layout must not write geometry and overgrow runs before the spawns exist, so cover lives in neither: it copies `scene_playable` into a new directory, `data/processed/scene_covered`, and `export package` reads that copy. `bench.py run --sightline-cover` sets the key in the run copies only. Off, the stage list, every argument and every file are those of a map without the key (the bench gives delta 0 and byte-identical files). The stage and directory are recorded here rather than in a new ADR.

**Targets.** `report.open_sight_points`: the route samples this ADR's report counts as open (decision 6) whose longest ray is strictly longer than `MAX_SIGHTLINE_M` = 60 m, longest first, then by position. The same routes, samples, 1.5 m reach and rays as the report; `build_report` is unchanged.

**Props.** Only installed rows of `assets/` that `read_library` loads (interior and kit rows refused), at street size (`fitted_props`), with a licence the legal record lists (the row's `license`, or CC0 1.0 for the Kenney catalogue, docs/legal/data-sources.md), of the roles barrier, barrel, wreck, debris and fence (never a building or a wall), anchored on the ground, solid by `overgrow.ambient.is_solid` and meeting the cover rule of decision 5 (`COVER_HEIGHT_M`, `COVER_DEPTH_M`, imported), at most 3 m long. Ranked by already in the scene (its material and draw are reused), then at least `SIGHT_BLOCK_HEIGHT_M` tall, then fewer triangles, then id. No asset is added.

**Spots.** For each target, 1 m then 0 m along the open ray, at 0, +1 and −1 m across it. The prop is turned 0 or 90 degrees, whichever lays its long side nearer across the ray: the report measures colliders by their axis-aligned box (decision 5), so an axis-aligned prop is measured as it stands. Its box lies within 1.5 m of the sample and at least 0.35 m from it. A target within 12 m of a placed prop, or with one within reach, is skipped.

**Keep-outs and passage.** A spot is kept only inside the core and on the navmesh islands; 5 m from every point `overgrow.dressing.load_spawns` reads (spawn points, AI starts, drives, Uplink, Escort, Evac, map quad); with its footprint on free walkable ground (no carriageway, no building); 3 m from an ammo box, a ladder or lift foot and a ground-floor door; 0.35 m from a Hunt route line and 1.5 m from its points; off a `detail` urban element; passing `overgrow.fit.object_reason`; overlapping no existing solid collider or earlier cover prop by more than 0.25 m². Overgrow's 4.2 m drive-lane rule is not applied: the footprint is kept off the carriageway itself, which is what the plan row asks. The passage is the ADR-031 test with `overgrow.fit.PASSAGE_M` = 1.2 m: the walkable ground within 6 m, less the solid colliders already there and the props placed so far, eroded by 0.6 m (less 1 mm, so exactly 1.2 m stays open), must keep every eroded place joined once the footprint is removed. The fit's own passage check reads only the nearest sidewalk lane, which misses a courtyard or a square. After the greedy pass the test runs on the whole playable ground; while it fails, or a spawn, the Uplink or an AI start leaves the islands, the last prop is dropped.

**Caps.** `MAX_COVER_PROPS` = 24; `COVER_MAX_ADDED_TRIS` = 12 000 and never more than overgrow left of its `MAX_ADDED_TRIS` = 80 000 (counted on the `overgrow/` nodes of the visual); 24 added primitives; 2 catalogue props not already in the scene.

**Output.** Visual nodes `overgrow/prop/<asset>/cover/<n>` in `scene_visual.glb` only, with the prop's own `prop_<asset>` materials (PP_Opaque_Simple_Lit, albedo only); boxes `overgrow/collider/prop/<asset>/cover/<n>` (`fit.collider_mesh`) in `scene_collision.glb` only; one `overgrowth` record each in `scene_detail.json`: origin `generated`, op `sightline_cover`, asset id, licence, position, yaw, size, `solid` and `reason` (`sample_m`, `ray_to_m`, `ray_length_m`). The geographic fingerprint and the attribution are unchanged, `overgrow fit`'s `check_scene` passes on the copy and `envelope.fit` names the new visual. `gameplay_report.json` is the report on the new collision file; `cover_report.json` (`onward_generator.cover_report` 0.1.0, `estimated`, `blocking: false`) holds the input hashes, the targets, candidates, placements with the rays each one shortens, rejects by reason, caps, the number of each keep-out, added triangles and primitives, and the headline and stretch counts before and after. No target, no prop or no spot copies the scene byte for byte and says why; a refusal exits 1 and leaves no directory. `layout.json` stays `gameplay_layout` 0.3.0 and the export `PAYLOAD` and adapter are unchanged.

**What the gain means.** The 2D navmesh ignores furniture (decision 3), so distinct routes, overlap and contact difference do not move. A sample with cover within 1.5 m stops counting as open even when the prop is lower than 1.2 m and the long view is not cut: part of the gain is by definition. `rays_cut` in `cover_report.json` counts the rays each prop really shortens. The headline is the longest open ray of the whole map, and 24 props cannot cover the many samples of a large map, so it falls less than the uncovered stretches do. The baked navmesh is the real one: a solid collider does cut it, which the passage test and the headset check below cover.
