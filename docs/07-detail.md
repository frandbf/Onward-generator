# Architectural and street detail (phase 04)

Module `onward_generator.detail`. Design decisions: `docs/decisions/ADR-004-facade-detail.md`. The tower volume is ADR-008.

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m onward_generator detail \
  --scene   maps/<id>/data/processed/geo/geo_scene.json \
  --heights maps/<id>/data/processed/heights/heights.json \
  --base    maps/<id>/data/processed/scene3d \
  --out     maps/<id>/data/processed/scene_detail \
  [--config config/detail.default.json] [--generation-config config/generation.default.json] \
  [--no-preview] [--overwrite]
```

Returns `0` when the automatic checks pass (no critical error) and `1` otherwise. The human visual review stays `pending` in the report. Passing the automatic checks does not close that review.

## Fidelity

Facades, roofs, and interiors are **generic templates** chosen by rules. They do not represent the real facade or interior of any building. Each building says so in the manifest: `representation: generic_template`, `representative_of_real_facade: false`, `attribute_origin: generated`.

- Landmarks (`landmark` in `heights.json`) get `landmark_placeholder`: a plain wall, one door, and `needs_custom_model: true`. No ornament is invented.
- **Tower.** If a landmark contains an OSM tower point (`man_made=tower`, `bell_tower`, and similar) with an observable `height` tag, a prism clipped to the building footprint is added. The height is **observed**. The tower square is **estimated** and never leaves the footprint. With no `height`, no volume is invented.
- **LoD2 volume** (plan D6, opt-in; [ADR-008](decisions/ADR-008-landmark-volumes.md) amendment, proposed). With `--lod2-landmarks`, a core landmark whose footprint the LoD2 index matches is drawn with that observed volume instead of the placeholder shell and its tower. See *LoD2 landmarks* below.
- Interiors use `representation: generic_simplified_interior` and `representative_of_real_interior: false`.
- The only observed inputs are what earlier stages already had: the OSM footprint, height and storeys (Catastro or OSM), the building category, and OSM POIs, plus the OSM [Simple 3D Buildings](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Simple_3D_Buildings) `roof:*`, `building:part`, `building:material`, `building:colour` and `roof:colour` tags read here (*Roofs* and *Parts* below; [ADR-023](decisions/ADR-023-simple-3d-buildings.md)). A commercial ground floor is applied only with evidence in `ground_floor_evidence`: an OSM POI (`shop`, `amenity`, `office`, or `craft`) inside the footprint, or within 2 m of it when the point is inside no footprint, or a building category that is already commercial, or an office or retail Catastro use (below). A point inside one building does not give its neighbour a shopfront across the party wall (ADR-016).
- **Catastro use** ([ADR-022](decisions/ADR-022-catastro-use.md)). A building whose best-ranked evidence is its `catastro_use` (`docs/05-cadastre.md`, *Current use*) has a `current_use` listed in `doors.shop_catastro_uses` (`4_1_office`, `4_2_retail`) also gets a `shopfront` ground floor, unless it is a landmark or no shop fits it: it needs an exposed street front, and a point inside its walls at least `wall_thickness_m + door_clearance_m / 2` from every wall (a part behind the others or a passage strip of the parcel holds no shop). The evidence ranking is `config/sources.json` `evidence` (`osm`, then `es.catastro`, then `overture`): with it, the OSM path is unchanged and wins, so a building with a POI keeps `source: osm_poi` and a commercial category keeps `building_category`. Were a plugin ranked above `osm`, a building it gives evidence keeps none of its POIs as shops. Catastro gives the predominant use of the parcel, so the use is observed and the floor it is put on is estimated: `ground_floor_evidence` is `{"source": "catastro_use", "osm_poi_ids": [], "current_use", "refcat", "share", "use_origin": "observed", "floor_assignment": "estimated", "rule", "provider", "license", "attribution"}`. A residential use, however many dwellings, gives nothing. The building is one shop with no POI (`poi_id: null`), named by the OSM `name` of the building when it has one, else by `shop_catastro_uses` (`Oficina`, `Comercio`; `name_origin: estimated`, `name_rule: catastro_use=<code>`). It is queued with the other shops, nearest the centre first, and never takes the stair interior. The facade use and its palette do not change. `counts.shopfront_evidence` in `detail_report.json` counts the shopfront buildings by source. Measured on Madrid (40.40657, −3.719187, r150, with shop rooms): 3 OSM-POI shops plus 3 from Catastro office parcels, 3 to 6 shop interiors (every shop has one), 15 to 18 lights, 91 823 to 91 965 drawn triangles.
- **Overture places** ([ADR-021](decisions/ADR-021-overture-evidence.md)). With `--places`, the Overture places inside a building (the same one-building-per-POI rule) are its evidence `overture`, ranked by `config/sources.json` `evidence` like the others: with the default ranking (`osm`, `es.catastro`, `overture`) only a building with no OSM POI, no commercial category and no qualifying Catastro use takes them. The places are its shops; the facade use and its palette do not change. The evidence is then `{"source": "overture_place", "overture_place_ids": [...]}`.
- **Overture colours** (plan C2, [ADR-021](decisions/ADR-021-overture-evidence.md) amendment; off by default). With `--overture-tints overture_tints.json` (written by `overture match --tints`, passed by `run` only with `map.json` `"overture_tints": true`), an observed Overture `facade_color` or `roof_color` lent to a matched OSM footprint is one more colour source, below every OSM wall and roof tag (*Materials* below). The file must be `onward_generator.overture_tints`, made for this scene's local grid, and name only buildings of the scene; otherwise, or when it is missing, `detail` exits 1.
- **LiDAR roof spread** (plan C3, [ADR-023](decisions/ADR-023-simple-3d-buildings.md) amendment; off by default). With `--lidar-roofs <id>_report.json` (the report a LiDAR source plugin's `source normalize` writes, passed by `run` only with `map.json` `"lidar_roofs": true`), the 25th–75th percentile spread of each footprint's nDSM cells decides a flat or pitched roof where no OSM roof tag does (*Roofs* below). The file must be `onward_generator.source_heights_report`, measured on this very scene (its `scene.sha256`), name only buildings of the scene, and hold numbers or `null` for `p25_m` and `p75_m` (in order) and counts for `valid` ≤ `cells`; otherwise, or when it is missing, `detail` exits 1 before writing anything.
- **One building per POI.** The shopfront evidence and the facade use (below) share one rule. A POI inside a footprint belongs to that building. A POI outside every footprint belongs to the nearest footprint within `shop_evidence_distance_m` (2 m). On a tie (a point on a party wall, or as far from two footprints) the footprint that contains the point wins, then the lowest building id. So a POI on a party line gives one building a shopfront, not both. Before this rule the shopfront took every footprint within 2 m and the facade use every footprint at the nearest distance: a POI 1 m in front of a party wall gave both neighbours a shopfront.

## Inputs

`--base` is the validated phase-03 directory:

- `generation_report.json` must have `phase_03_exit.passed`;
- the recorded SHA-256 of `geo_scene.json`, `heights.json`, and the generation configuration must match;
- phase 03 is rebuilt in memory and the manifest must match the file on disk. Inside `run`, the rebuild is the copy kept from `generate` (`docs/06-generation.md`, Inputs).

A mismatch raises `DetailError` and writes nothing.

`config/detail.default.json` is validated with `config/detail.schema.json`. Every value is a modelling assumption, not an observation. Examples: every window or balcony fits its bay with a minimum pier; each category has one template; every material exists.

## What is built

### Facades

1. **Exposure.** A 1 m probe outside each edge is intersected with neighbouring footprints. An edge that touches a neighbour is a blind party wall. An exposed run shorter than 2.5 m is also blind. Cuts are inserted as collinear vertices, so the area does not change (`refined_footprint_area_changed` is critical). Interior courtyards get no openings.
2. **Storeys.** If `heights.json` has `estimated_levels`, the storey height is h/n. Otherwise 3 m is used, clamped between 2.6 m and 6 m. The origin is stored in `storeys.origin`. A building that receives an interior and whose storey is below 2.8 m is raised to 2.8 m per storey when the height is an estimate. An OSM `height`, a Catastro height, or Catastro levels stay as recorded. The previous height is kept on the building as `previous_height_m`, with `interior_storey_origin: estimated`. `heights.json` is not rewritten.
3. **Bays.** Each exposed run is split into bays of `bay_m`. The ground floor is `door_and_windows`, `shopfront` when there is evidence, or `main_door_only` on landmarks. The main door sits in the centre bay of the longest exposed run. On a `shopfront` ground floor it is a `shop_door` (see *Shopfronts and shop signs*). Upper floors are windows or balcony stacks. The choice is `sha256(seed, building, run, column) < balcony.share`. An opening is placed only when its recess stays inside the footprint. A balcony is placed only when it does not enter another footprint.
   **A door on every building.** When the template places no door (the storey is too low for the 2.4 m door under its 0.35 m lintel, or no wall reaches the 2.5 m street-facing minimum), a fallback door is cut. The walls are tried in this order: the street-facing walls, longest first (the first is the main frontage of a one-part building), then outer walls too short to count as street-facing whose 1 m (`probe_m`) strip outside meets no other footprint. A party wall is never used. The door sits in the centre bay of that wall. It keeps the template size when it fits and shrinks to the wall (a 0.5 m pier each side) and to the storey (minus the lintel), but not below 0.9 × 2.0 m. It replaces the ground-floor openings within 0.5 m of it. The building record says how the door came: `facade.door` is `template`, `fallback`, or `none`. `facade.doors_m` (added 5 November 2026) lists the middle of each ground-floor door on the wall line, `[x, y]` in metres to the millimetre, in the same frame as an interior's `connections[].position_m`: every `door`, `shop_door`, `landmark_door` and `interior_door` (a shop room's own door) on the ground floor. A scenic building has none. The layout keeps ladders and lift cabins 3 m from them (ADR-029). A manifest written before has no `doors_m`, and readers treat it as empty. A shop gets the fallback too, as a `shop_door` (glazed, 1.6 × 2.5 m when it fits), so a shop on a low storey still has an entrance. Measured on the six-building test row (`ROW_BUILDINGS` in `tests/detail_fixture.py`): 3 of 6 buildings had a door before, 6 of 6 after; the sixth, a shop on 2.67 m storeys, gets a 1.6 x 2.32 m shop door.
4. **Geometry.** Openings are exact holes in the wall face, plus notches for doors. GEOS boolean is not used. Each opening has a 0.15 m recess: jambs, sill and head in the wall's own material (since 11 November 2026, plan Q6; before, in `frame_stone`, whose near-white sunlit jambs read as strips floating off a darker facade on Rue Mouffetard) and a back of `glass_dark` or `door_wood` (a shop door is glazed: `glass_dark`). On a building with an interior, a window below the accessible ceiling uses the full wall thickness and a single `glass_view` quad. The wall and its recesses are a closed solid. The only open edges are the base (z = 0) and the through-door of an interior.
   **Flat upper openings** ([ADR-025](decisions/ADR-025-flat-upper-openings.md)). A `window`, `narrow_window` or `balcony_door` with a `glass_dark` back on storey `openings.relief_storeys` (default 2) or above has no recess: no jambs, no sill, no head, no back 0.15 m in. Its rectangle is one quad (2 triangles) in the back material, in the wall plane, filling the hole the wall face keeps, so the two share every edge of the opening, do not overlap, and the shell stays closed (the same `facade_mesh_not_watertight` check). Storeys count from 0 at the ground, so the default keeps the ground and first floors in relief, where a player sees the reveal. Doors, shop doors, shopfronts, interior doors and every opening an interior pierces (`glass_view`, `shop_glass`) stay recessed on any storey. Balcony slabs and railings do not change. `0` flattens every window and balcony door, the ground floor included; a value above the tallest building (for example 1000) gives the recessed geometry of before, byte for byte. The layout does not change: the same openings are placed, with the same footprint check on a 0.15 m recess. Each building record has `facade.relief`: `{"relief_storeys", "flat_openings", "recessed_openings"}` (every opening the shell drew; balconies are not openings), and `counts.flat_openings` in `detail_report.json` sums the flat ones. Under the trim sheet (ADR-020) a flat back maps to its window cell as a recessed back did; the collider never followed a recess and does not change. Measured with `run --no-unity` and `perf measure --combine-cell-m 100` on the export: Madrid (40.40657, −3.719187, r150) 2 156 of 3 321 openings flat, `frame_stone` 36 854 → 19 606 triangles, export 142 408 → 125 160; Murcia (37.99521, −1.137511, r90) 622 of 1 028 flat, export 62 714 → 57 738. Materials and predicted draws do not change.
   **Setbacks.** A Catastro split (ADR-016) makes every part of a parcel its own sub-building, so a Madrid setback (an *ático* over a terrace) or a taller rear wing stands against a lower part of the same parcel. A party wall of a building is a **setback wall** when every footprint its 1 m probe meets is a part of the same parcel (same `subdivision.refcat`; without one, the same OSM `parent`) and the highest roof of those parts is below the building's own roof. The bays of step 3 are laid on it too, with that roof as the floor: a storey whose top is not above the floor is skipped, and no opening starts below it. A terrace parapet may hide a sill, as on a real setback. A balcony still never enters another footprint, so over a terrace the bay takes a window. A wall that meets another parcel anywhere stays a blank party wall: a *medianera* is blank in reality too. `counts.setback_walls` in `detail_report.json` counts the setback walls (`walls`), those that took an opening (`opened`), and the openings (`openings`). On Madrid (40.40657, −3.719187, r150: 9 OSM blocks split into 72 sub-buildings, each block one parcel) there are 442 setback walls, 217 open with 429 openings; most of the others are shorter than a window bay (253 of the 442 are under 3 m).
   **Party walls.** Once every shell is built, each blank party wall of a core-tier building (a party segment with no opening) is split by what stands against it. Where a neighbour footprint lies within `HIDDEN_GAP_M` (5 cm) outside the wall, the wall is hidden up to the lower of the two wall tops (`z_top`, the parapet included): nobody can see it, and it is left out of the visual mesh. The rest of the wall, above a lower neighbour or where no neighbour touches (a gap of more than 5 cm, a blind run shorter than 2.5 m), stays in the visual mesh with the material `party_<wall>`: the same colour and albedo as the building's wall, under its own name so the export can bake it at its own lightmap scale (`docs/12-export.md`). A 5 cm seam between two neighbours is closed, so it leaves no sliver. The face is rebuilt from exact coordinates, one polygon per stretch the neighbours leave open. A party wall with openings (a setback wall, or a fallback door on a free short wall) loses the band below its lowest opening that the neighbours hide along its whole length, and keeps the rest, openings included, in the wall material. The facade check above (`facade_mesh_not_watertight`) runs on the whole shell before the split, so the visual shell is open only where a neighbour's wall closes it. Only core shells take part: a scenic shell (ADR-017) keeps its own plain walls and hides no core wall. The collider is the phase-03 prism or the interior shell and is not touched. Each building record has `facade.wall_area_m2` (`street`, `party`, `hidden`: gross areas in m², openings not subtracted; `street` also holds a party wall with openings above its hidden band), and `counts.wall_area_m2` in `detail_report.json` sums them. `detail` and `config_from_document` add a `party_<name>` copy of every material in `/materials`. Before this change every party wall was drawn whole in the wall material and baked like a street facade. Measured on a synthetic 94-building map of Catastro-like lots (12 blocks of 40 × 40 m, 1 to 7 storeys, touching lots): 30 804 m² street, 6 146 m² party, 16 912 m² hidden, so 31 % of the wall area left the visual mesh. The visual triangles went from 53 964 to 53 816 (a hidden wall was two triangles; a wall cut by its neighbours is a few more), the materials from 21 to 26 (one `party_<wall>` per wall material in use), and the renderers combined by 100 m cell and material from 64 to 84.
5. **Roofs.** An OSM `roof:shape` is drawn on a footprint of any area (`detail/roofs.py`, written from the Simple 3D Buildings description, no renderer's code). The roof's `origin` is `observed`.

   | `roof:shape` | Drawn as (`roof.type`) |
   | --- | --- |
   | `gabled` | `gabled`: two slopes; the ridge runs along the longest side of the minimum rotated rectangle, through its middle, or across it with `roof:orientation=across`, or square to `roof:direction` |
   | `hipped` | `hipped`: four faces of one pitch on the minimum rotated rectangle, the ridge half a width short of each end, when the footprint fills at least 95% of that rectangle; otherwise `pyramidal` |
   | `half-hipped` | `half-hipped`: a gable along the rectangle whose ends are gables up to half the rise and hips above it, every face of one pitch, so each end wall is a trapezoid; on the same 95% rule, otherwise `gabled` |
   | `skillion` | `skillion`: one slope falling towards `roof:direction` (a compass bearing or `N`, `SE`, …), or across the long side without it |
   | `pyramidal` | `pyramidal`: a fan from the centroid to one apex |
   | `dome` | `dome`: three rings scaled about the centroid on a quarter circle, and an apex |
   | `mansard` | `hipped` |
   | `gambrel`, `saltbox`, `round` | `gabled` |
   | `onion` | `dome` |
   | `cone` | `pyramidal` |
   | `flat` | the template roof |
   | any other value | the template roof, and `roof_shape_unsupported` (info). No estimated hip is drawn over a tag |

   The rise is `roof:height`, else `roof:levels` × the building's storey height, else `tan(roof:angle)` × the half span; with none of them, a 30° pitch capped at 4 m (`rise_origin: estimated`; a dome rises its inradius, at most 15 m). `roof.rise_rule` names the source. A shape drawn as another records `approximation`. Gabled and skillion roofs record `direction_origin` (`observed` with `roof:direction` or `roof:orientation`). The roof sits on top of the building's recorded height, as the template hip did: phase 03 extrudes that height, and its collider stays the phase-03 prism (see *Limits* below).
   **How it is built.** The roof is a height field over the footprint: the footprint is cut along the lines where the slope changes (the ridge, the hips), each piece is triangulated flat and lifted to the field, and every wall edge the roof leaves above the eaves gets a vertical gable piece in the wall material. The pieces keep the footprint's own vertices (a cut that comes back within 1e-7 m of a corner is snapped onto it), so the shell stays watertight (`facade_mesh_not_watertight` still checks it). A gabled roof costs about one triangle per footprint vertex plus two per gable end; a 12 × 8 m house is 4 + 2.
   **Fallbacks.** A footprint with a courtyard, of several parts, of more than 64 vertices, narrower than 1 m across the slope, with a rise under 0.1 m, or (pyramidal and dome) whose centroid does not see every eave, gets a flat roof with `roof.fallback` set to the reason and `roof_shape_fallback` (info).
   `roof:colour`, then `roof:material`, chooses the material (*Materials*). With no tag, a convex footprint of at most 160 m² that the template gives a parapet may receive an estimated `hip` (`origin: estimated`), unless a taller part of its own parcel stands within `probe_m`: that roof is the terrace under a setback and keeps its flat roof and parapet. The roof grammar and the flat roof material are drawn for the parcel (`sha256(seed, parcel, …)`, the key of *Choice* below), so the parts of one parcel share them. Otherwise the template stands: `flat_parapet` (slab, parapet, coping), `hip`, or `flat`. A hip is a fan from the footprint's centroid, so it is kept flat when the centroid does not see every eave from inside: a jog of a few centimetres in a near-convex footprint (common where a Catastro split meets the street) would fold one face under the roof. The projected roof area is checked against the footprint (`roof_area_mismatch`). `counts.roof_shapes` in `detail_report.json` counts `<roof:shape>:<roof.type>`.
   **From the nDSM spread** (`--lidar-roofs`, plan C3, off by default; `detail/lidar_roofs.py`). Each building is classified in this order, the first check that fires naming the result: an OSM `roof:shape`, `roof:height`, `roof:levels` or `roof:angle` → `tagged` (the tag is drawn as above); a scenic shell → `scenic`; the terrace under a taller part of its own parcel → `terrace`; no `measured` entry with both percentiles → `no_data`; fewer than 8 valid cells → `too_few_cells`; `p75 − p25` at most 0.6 m → **`flat`**; footprint under 85 % of its minimum rotated rectangle → `not_rectangular`; `s = spread / half span` (half the short side of that rectangle) over 0.5 → `mixed` (a step) or under 0.1 → `ambiguous`; otherwise **`pitched`**. A gable of pitch *a* gives *s* ≈ tan(*a*)/2, so 0.1–0.5 is about 11–45°. A `flat` building keeps its template roof and parapet and draws no hip, neither an estimated one nor its template's (the `generic_minor` template has one). A `pitched` one gets a `gabled` roof on any area, its ridge along the long side of the minimum rotated rectangle, rise = the spread within 0.1–4 m (`rise_rule: ndsm_spread`, `rise_origin: estimated`), on top of the recorded height as every roof here. The recorded LiDAR median lies between eave and ridge, so the drawn ridge lands near the measured one. Its material is a `roof:colour`/`roof:material` tag or a lent Overture colour (as a `roof_tile` tint), else `roof_tile`. A gable that does not fit falls back flat (no parapet), `roof_shape_fallback` says "the estimated gable (nDSM spread) drawn flat: <reason>", and it counts as `fallback`. A classified roof has `roof.origin: estimated` and `roof.lidar` (`source`, `provider`, `license`, `p25_m`, `p75_m`, `valid`, `cells`, `rule: ndsm_spread`, `class`, `origin: estimated`); `facade.materials.roof_origin` stays the material's. A pitched roof gets no roof access (ADR-029). `detail_report.json` gains `inputs.roof_spread` (`path`, `sha256`, `source`) and `counts.lidar_roofs` (`source`, `buildings`, `core_buildings`, `flat`, `pitched`, `unclassified` by `tagged`, `scenic`, `terrace`, `no_data`, `too_few_cells`, `not_rectangular`, `mixed`, `ambiguous`, `fallback`), and the attribution the plugin's line when at least one roof was classified. Without the flag none of these keys is written and the outputs are byte-identical. Measured on the bench (Murcia 2.6 % and Madrid 3.3 % of core buildings classified): [09-perf.md](09-perf.md#roof-form-from-the-ndsm-spread).
   Before this change only a footprint of at most 800 m² took its tag, and every shape became the centroid fan (`hip`). Colours were ignored.
   Measured with `detail` on the same phase-03 base before and after this change (150 m radius, every building in the core): Madrid (40.40657, −3.719187, with and without Catastro) has no `roof:shape` inside its footprints and does not change. Kraków (50.0614, 19.9366) draws 37 of its 47 pitched tags (35 gabled, 1 pyramidal, 1 hipped as a pyramid) instead of 5 centroid fans; the 10 left flat are 8 courtyards and 2 jogs the centroid cannot see. Its visual triangles go from 99 358 to 100 840 (+1.5%); collision and materials do not change.
6. **Materials.** OSM `building:colour` wins, then `building:material` or `material` (`wall_origin: observed`). A colour (a CSS name or `#rgb`/`#rrggbb`) is a **tint**, not a texture (ADR-023): it is quantized in CIELAB to the nearest of 12 wall tints (white, cream, yellow, ochre, orange, pink, red, brown, grey, dark, green, blue; `WALL_PALETTE` in `detail/s3db.py`) and the wall takes `<base>__tint_<key>`, a shared copy of the base material with its texture and roughness and the tint as its colour. The base is the texture `building:material` names when it is `brick_red`, `stone_warm` or `stone_light`, else `plaster_white`: `building:colour=blue` gives `plaster_white__tint_blue`, a white brick building `brick_red__tint_white`. `roof:colour` takes one of 7 roof tints (red, brown, grey, dark, light, green, blue) over the material `roof:material` names, else over `roof_tile` under a pitched `roof:shape` and `roof_flat_grey` otherwise; it wins over `roof:material`. The configuration defines every tint of every base it has (and a `party_` copy of each), and the GLB carries only those in use, so colours add at most 48 wall and 14 roof materials and in practice a few (ADR-005). `facade.materials` names the tag in `wall_source` and `roof_source` (`null` when generated).
   With `--overture-tints` (plan C2), a colour lent by Overture comes after the OSM tags. For a wall it comes after `building:colour` and `building:material`/`material` (a material value the table does not know names nothing) and before the palette, as a tint of `plaster_white`. For a roof it comes after `roof:colour` and `roof:material` and before the template, as a tint of `roof_tile` under a pitched `roof:shape` and of `roof_flat_grey` otherwise. An estimated hip keeps its tile, as under `roof:colour`. The record then says `wall_source: "overture:facade_color"` or `roof_source: "overture:roof_color"` with origin `observed`. It adds `wall_evidence` or `roof_evidence`: `provider: overture`, `gers_id`, `dataset`, `license`, `origin: observed`, `colour` and `iou`. A landmark keeps its walls. Building parts and scenic shells take no lent colour. The parts of one Catastro parcel share it: the first part in id order with a colour lends it to all of them. The visual node of a building whose tints all came from Overture carries `tint_source: "overture"`. `detail_report.json` gains `counts.third_party_tints` (`matched`, `walls`, `roofs`, `by_dataset`, and `not_used` by `osm_tag`, `landmark`, `scenic`, `estimated_roof` and `no_tint_material`). The attribution gains one line per Overture dataset whose colour was used. Without the flag no key is written and the scene is byte-identical. Otherwise each building draws its wall from the palette of its **facade use** (`facade_palettes` in the configuration), and the record says `wall_origin: generated`. A landmark keeps its template wall (`stone_warm`).
   Measured with `detail` on the same phase-03 base before and after the colour tints (150 m radius, every building in the core): Madrid (40.40657, −3.719187, with and without Catastro) has no colour tag inside its footprints and does not change. Kraków (50.0614, 19.9366) tints 84 walls (51 buildings, 33 parts) and 45 roofs (43, 2) in 9 tints; its materials go from 40 to 55 (9 tints and 6 `party_` copies of the wall tints) and its triangles do not change. Both maps were already over the provisional `max_materials` of 32 (`budget_exceeded`, a warning).
   - **Use.** Read from the building's own tags first, then from a POI inside the footprint, then from an area that holds the footprint's representative point (a school ground), then from a POI outside every footprint whose nearest footprint within `shop_evidence_distance_m` (2 m) is this one (*One building per POI*, above). Street furniture POIs are not evidence. When several tags name a use, the most specific wins: `historic`, `institutional`, `office`, `retail`, `industrial`, `residential`. A building with no evidence is `residential`, `origin: estimated`. A POI near the footprint is also `estimated`; the building's tags, a POI inside, and an enclosing area are `observed`. The record keeps it as `facade.use`: `{"use", "origin", "evidence": [{"source_id", "tag", "match"}]}`, with `match` one of `building`, `poi_inside`, `enclosing_area`, `poi_near`, or `landmark`.

     | Use | OSM tags (`building` and `building:use` read alike) | Palette |
     | --- | --- | --- |
     | `institutional` | `building` school, kindergarten, university, college, public, civic, government, townhall, hospital, fire_station, education; `amenity` school, kindergarten, college, university, library, townhall, courthouse, police, hospital, clinic, fire_station, community_centre; `office=government` | brick, white plaster, cream plaster |
     | `office` | `building` office, commercial, bank; `amenity` bank, bureau_de_change; any other `office`; `tourism=hotel` | warm stone, white plaster, cream plaster |
     | `retail` | `building` retail, supermarket, kiosk; any `shop`; `amenity` restaurant, cafe, bar, pub, fast_food, pharmacy, ice_cream | ochre, cream, salmon, white plaster (above the shopfront) |
     | `historic` | `historic` building, castle, palace, manor, monastery; `tourism` museum, gallery | cream plaster, warm stone |
     | `industrial` | `building` industrial, warehouse, manufacture | white plaster, brick |
     | `residential` | `building` apartments, residential, house, detached, semidetached_house, terrace, dormitory, bungalow; no evidence | ochre, cream, salmon, white plaster, brick |
   - **Choice.** Buildings are taken in id order. Each ranks its palette by `sha256(seed, building, "wall", material)` and takes the first material that no footprint within `probe_m` already has. The parts of one parcel (ADR-016) are one building here: the key is `parcel:<refcat>` (a sub-building without a parcel reference uses its OSM `parent` id), the first part in id order resolves the use from the evidence of every part and draws the wall avoiding the walls around any part, and the other parts take the same use and wall. Before this rule each part drew its own wall and avoided its touching siblings, so a setback over a street facade came out in another colour (seen on the headset in Madrid). A building outside a split keeps its own id as the key, so its wall does not change. When every material is taken, it keeps its first. So two buildings that touch get different walls whenever the palette has a second material. The palette is a fixed set of shared materials, not one per building, so the draw calls stay bounded (ADR-005). `counts.facade_uses` and `counts.wall_materials` in `detail_report.json` count them.
   - Before this rule a residential building drew from its template's two plasters (ochre, cream), with stone and brick as rare estimates, each building on its own. Touching buildings matched half the time, so a street block often read as one texture: on the test block the three touching buildings 311, 312, and 313 were all ochre. They are now white, cream, and salmon.
7. **Parts.** An OSM `building:part` (a closed way, which `geo` keeps as a line, or a polygon) is extruded, clipped to the building it overlaps most, with its own collision. A way that is not closed (cut at the map edge) is `building_part_not_closed` (info).
   - **Height.** The Simple 3D Buildings `height` includes the roof. Without `height`, the part is `(building:levels + roof:levels) × storey height`, the storey height of the clipping building's category in `heights.json` (`assumptions.storey_height_m`): `height.origin: estimated`, and `height.rule` writes the sum out. The base is `min_height`, else `building:min_level` (or `min_level`) × the storey height (`min_height_origin`). With neither `height` nor `building:levels` nothing is invented (`building_part_no_height`).
   - **Hidden parts.** A part no higher than the building it is clipped to lies inside that building's walls and is not drawn (`building_part_hidden`, info). A higher part draws its walls only from that building's top (or its own base, if higher) to its eaves; a roof that starts lower keeps no wall. Its collider still runs from its base. Before 5 November 2026 the walls started at the base: where the part's outline runs on its building's, they stood over that facade, and on Barcelona Sarria they hid 11 shopfronts with their vinyls and z-fought with the walls they covered. On an edge that runs on the building's outline (within 1 cm of it along its whole length, `PART_OUTLINE_M`) the part's wall starts at the top of the building's own wall, its parapet included, so the 0.7 m of a flat parapet is drawn once, by the building. Until 5 November 2026 the part's wall started at the roof there too and z-fought with the parapet. An inner wall beside such an edge gets a vertex at the parapet top on that side, so the two walls share their vertical edge.
   - **Roof.** `roof:*` on the part draws its roof as in 5, under its `height`: the walls stop at `height.eave_m` = height − rise, and the rise is cut so at least 0.5 m of wall remains above the base. The collider is the prism from the base to the eaves. `roof:colour` and `building:colour` choose the materials as in 6.
   - `counts.building_parts` in `detail_report.json` gives `kept`, `height_origin`, `roofs` and `skipped` by issue code.
   Before this change `geo` handed every part over as a line, and a line was not read as a polygon, so no part was ever drawn: Madrid's parts (which carry `building:levels` and no `height`) were reported `building_part_no_height`, and Kraków's 144 parts with `height` were dropped without an issue.

   Measured with `detail` on the same phase-03 base before and after this change (150 m radius, every building in the core). Madrid (40.40657, −3.719187, with and without Catastro): 0 parts kept before and after. Its 38 parts lie in the margin near 40.4079, −3.7211, away from any outline in the extract: 25 outside a footprint, 8 cut at the edge, 1 hidden, 4 under 1 m²; triangles do not change. Kraków (50.0614, 19.9366): 0 → 35 parts kept (33 from `height`, 2 from levels; 4 gabled, 1 hipped and 1 pyramidal roof), 32 hidden inside their building, 22 outside, 5 cut at the edge. Visual triangles 100 840 → 101 765, collision 80 052 → 81 124, materials unchanged.
8. **Wall texture frame.** Each building node in `scene_visual.glb` carries `extras.uv_frame` ([ADR-012](decisions/ADR-012-wall-uvs.md)): the exposed runs (chains of street-facing segments, broken by party walls, as `[ax, ay, bx, by]` in ring order), `storey_h`, and `storeys_origin` (a copy of `storeys.origin`). Without `estimated_levels`, or on a landmark, `storey_h` and `storeys_origin` are `null`. No coordinates are written here, and `scene_detail.json` does not change. `dress style` and `materials apply` turn the frame into UVs (`docs/08-dress.md`).
10. **Interior light anchors.** Each `interior/<building>` node in `scene_visual.glb` carries `extras.light_anchors` ([ADR-013](decisions/ADR-013-interior-light-anchors.md)), `attribute_origin: generated`. One entry per furnished room: `room_id`, `light_m` (the room centroid, or a point clear of the stair wells, 0.3 m under the ceiling, in local metres), `range_m` (half the room diagonal plus 1 m, 4–12 m), and `probes_m` (flat x, y, z triples: a grid of at most 4 × 4 columns, 0.5 m off the walls and out of the wells, at 1 m above the floor and 0.5 m under the ceiling). `scene_detail.json` does not change. The adapter turns the anchors into baked lights and light probes (`docs/12-export.md`). The map keeps at most `interiors.lights_max` (160) lights: see *Shop interiors*.
9. **Generated furniture.** Besides lamps, benches and bins are placed along pedestrian ways at a fixed spacing, from the seed, with a cap per type. An OSM point (`amenity=bench`, `amenity=waste_basket`, `natural=tree`, and similar) still places the observed object.

### Typology

[ADR-024](decisions/ADR-024-building-typology.md). Each building records `facade.typology`, and its facade style follows from it (below). The rules are in `config/typology.default.json` (schema beside it; `detail --typology-config`), and every threshold is a modelling assumption.

- **Era.** Observed or nothing, never estimated. `config/sources.json` `evidence` ranks the sources: an OSM date (`building:start_date`, `start_date`, `construction_date`: a leading four-digit year, as in `1890-05-01`, `~1890`, `1960s`), then the Catastro `construction_year`. Overture has no year. A date with no leading year (`C19`, `before 1900`) is `era_unparsable` (info) and gives no era. Periods: `pre_1900`, `1900_1939`, `1940_1959`, `1960_1979`, `1980_1999`, `2000_plus`.
- **Morphology.** In the scene's metric CRS: area, perimeter, compactness (4πA/P²), elongation (long over short side of the minimum rotated rectangle), and the shared-wall share: the share of the outer ring that the facade probe (`probe_m`) finds against another building. An exposed run under 0.1 m is the end of a probe and is not counted as exposed; a real gap between two neighbours is.
- **One record per parcel.** The parts of a Catastro parcel (the key of *Choice*) share one record, measured on their union, against the footprints of other keys. Height and storeys are those of the tallest part.
- **Rule.** The first that matches names the typology:

  | Order | Typology | Rule |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 1 | `landmark` | a part is a landmark in `heights.json` |
  | 2 | `other` | the category is minor |
  | 3 | `civic` | use `institutional` or `historic`, Catastro `public_services`, or category `public` or `religious` |
  | 4 | `industrial_shed` | use, Catastro use or category industrial; or at most 1 storey and at least 800 m² |
  | 5 | `tower` | at least 13 storeys |
  | 6 | `historic_core` | attached (share ≥ 0.1), era before 1940 |
  | 7 | `row_house`, `detached_house` | at most 3 storeys and 250 m²; attached or not |
  | 8 | `closed_block` | share ≥ 0.3, 2 to 12 storeys |
  | 9 | `open_block` | share < 0.3, 4 to 12 storeys, at least 150 m²; a slab with elongation ≥ 2, a point block with compactness ≥ 0.6 |
  | 10 | `other` | no rule |

- **Facade style** ([ADR-027](decisions/ADR-027-typology-facades.md)). `facades` in the same file tunes each building's template: `bay_factor`, `window_width_factor`, `window_height_factor` (0.7–1.4) and `balcony_share`, and for a residential use the wall `palette`. The values of its typology are read first and those of its era go over them, because the era is the stronger evidence. The neighbour rule of *Choice* draws from the palette, and an observed `building:colour` or `building:material` still wins. A landmark keeps its walls, and its template has no windows to tune. Layout still checks that every opening fits, so an oversized window falls back to the narrow one or to no opening. The facade stays a generic template (`representative_of_real_facade: false`).
- **Record.** `{"typology", "origin": "estimated", "rule", "inputs": {...}, "era": {"year", "period", "origin": "observed", "source", "evidence"} | null, "facade_style": {...}}`. The typology is always `estimated`; the era keeps its own origin. With the opt-in regional atlases (`materials apply --regional-facades`, plan D1) the trim pass reads an `observed` era to choose the building's era band, an estimate recorded as such in `materials_report.json` `facades.era_bands`; an era that is missing or not observed changes nothing. `counts.typologies`, `counts.eras` and `counts.era_sources` in `detail_report.json` count core buildings, and `run` prints `typology` and `era` lines.

### Shopfronts and shop signs

Only a building with a `shopfront` ground floor (the evidence above) gets these. Configuration: `doors.shopfront`, `doors.shop_door` and `shops` in `config/detail.default.json`.

- **Display window.** Each ground-floor bay that is not the entrance gets one `shopfront`: as wide as the bay less `min_pier_m`, at most `max_width_m` (3.4 m), and as tall as the storey less `sill_m` (0.25 m, a stallriser) and `head_m` (0.45 m, room for the fascia), at most `max_height_m` (3.2 m). Below `min_width_m` (2.0 m) or `min_height_m` (1.9 m) the bay gets a window instead. On a 3 m storey and a 3.6 m bay that is 3.1 × 2.3 m (7.1 m²); the fixed 2.4 × 2.5 m opening it replaces was 6.0 m². A taller storey gets a taller window. The back is `glass_dark`, or the frameless `shop_glass` when the shop interior pierces it (see *Shop interiors*).
- **Shop door.** The entrance of a shop is a `shop_door`, 1.1 × 2.3 m (1.6 × 2.5 m before 12 November 2026, too large on the headset) with a `glass_dark` back, where a home gets a 1.2 × 2.4 m `door` in `door_wood`. It is a recess like the plain door: it is not walkable and the collider stays the phase-03 prism. A shop that gets its interior has the walkable `interior_door` (1.2 × 2.4 m, open) in its place.
- **Shops.** Each evidence POI is a shop. A commercial building with no POI is one shop. A shop takes the free shopfront nearest to its POI. A shop left without one gets no sign.
- **Name.** The POI `name` tag (`name_origin: observed`). Without it, `shops.generic_names` gives a Spanish word for `shop=<value>`, `amenity=<value>`, … in the order of `shop_evidence_keys`, then for the key alone (`shop` → `Tienda`), else `default_name` (`Comercio`). Those carry `name_origin: estimated` and the rule used in `name_rule`. An Overture place's name is its `names.primary` (`name_origin: observed`), and the shop records `poi_source`: `provider: overture`, `gers_id`, `dataset`, `license`, `confidence`, `attribute_origin: observed`, `observed_by: third_party`. The manifest attribution gains one line per dataset whose places gave a shop, and `counts.third_party_shops` in `detail_report.json` gives `places`, `buildings`, `shops` and `by_dataset`.
- **Sign.** One per shop. A fascia when `sha256(seed, building, shop, shop_sign) < fascia_share` (0.5), otherwise a blade sign. The fascia is a 0.36 × 0.06 m board 5 cm above the window head, 0.15 m wider than the window on each side. Under a balcony slab of the same wall that reaches over the board, it comes down until its top is 5 cm below the slab, over the window head if it must: on Barcelona Sarria (5 November 2026) 18 of 40 fascias had their top 0.11 m inside the first-floor slab and their letters clipped. The blade is a 0.7 × 0.5 m board, 4 cm thick, perpendicular to the wall, 0.15 m from it on a bracket, its bottom at 2.4 m, in the pier beside the window. No balcony or upper window reaches that pier. The semantic checks keep the fascia under `head_m` and both boards inside the pier. A blade whose faces look at a footprint within 1 m (`BLADE_SIDE_CLEAR_M`), across the board's width, is a fascia instead: the faces look along the wall, and on Barcelona Sarria blades in re-entrant corners had one face 0.7 to 0.8 m from a neighbour's wall. The footprints are all of the map's, the building's own included. On Barcelona Sarria with its 100 m core 4 of 14 blades become fascias.
- **Vinyl.** A shopfront with a `glass_dark`, `glass_view` or `shop_glass` back gets the name of the nearest shop when `sha256(seed, building, segment, column, vinyl) < vinyl_share` (0.5). It is 80% of the window wide, 0.26 m tall, centred at 1.6 m (kept on the window), 1 cm in front of the glass; on the see-through pane of a shop interior that is inside the wall, 1 cm on the street side of the pane. Each vinyl records the `glass` it sits on. On `shop_glass` it is a decal: black letters with nothing around them (below). On the other backs it is the opaque strip. Any other back (`glass_boarded`, a door) gets none.

`facade.shops` in `scene_detail.json` lists, per shop: `id`, `poi_id`, `name`, `name_origin`, `name_rule`, `sign` (`kind`, `centre_m`, `normal`, `width_m`, `height_m`, `depth_m`, `double_sided`, `bracket_m`, `attribute_origin: generated`, or `null`) and `vinyls` (same frame, depth 0, plus `glass`: the back it sits on). Buildings without a shopfront have an empty list. Phase 04 draws nothing for them: `signs place` builds the boards and the text atlas (`docs/01-pipeline.md`, 8b).

Cost per shop: a fascia is 10 triangles (front and four rims; the back is against the wall), a blade 20 (two faces, four rims, a four-sided bracket), a vinyl 2. The boards and the strips share one atlas material `shop_sign_atlas_0` (2048 px wide, 200 px per metre, a second page only when the first is full), the decals their own `shop_decal_atlas_0` (same packing, same limits), and every rim and bracket shares `shop_sign_edge`: three materials for the whole map. The strip is opaque: an opaque `glass_dark` pane behind it hides nothing, so it takes the glass colour of the window image behind white letters. The decal page is RGBA: the letters are black (16, 16, 18) and opaque, and everything else has alpha 0, with the ink colour in its RGB so a mipmap blends no light halo into the letters. The adapter draws the decal pages with `PP_Sprite_Lit`, both faces, one render queue step after the shop glass, so from the street the letters sit on the glass and the room shows around and between them; from inside they read mirrored, as a real vinyl does. Before this change every vinyl was the opaque strip, and on a see-through pane it was a dark band with white letters across the glass. Signs add no collider. A fascia takes about 1.2 m² of lightmap and a blade about 0.7 m².

### Shop interiors

Every building with a `shopfront` ground floor (the evidence above) gets a generic shop storey behind its shopfronts, in place of the stair interior: one room per shop on its main frontage (see *Rooms*). Configuration: `interiors.shops` and `interiors.lights_max` in `config/detail.default.json`. Representation `generic_simplified_interior`, `representative_of_real_interior: false`, `attribute_origin: generated`: nothing about the real shop is known.

- **One storey.** The plan is the stair interior's with `max_floors = 1`: one open room over the whole inner footprint (the footprint less `wall_thickness_m`), the walkable `interior_door` (1.2 × 2.4 m) on the main frontage, the walls, the floor, and a ceiling at the first storey line. There is no stair, no upper floor and no roof access. Above the ceiling the collider is solid (`upper_solid`) and the upper windows stay `glass_dark`. A one-storey shop (a single storey in `heights.json`, often a Catastro sub-building, ADR-016) has its room under the roof: the ceiling is the underside of a `roof_slab` collider `slab_m` (0.2 m) thick under the roof line, there is nothing solid above it, and the shell's roof (flat or hip) is unchanged and stays closed. The stair interiors still need two storeys. The storey is raised to 2.8 m when the height is an estimate, as for any interior. The shop takes neither the quota nor the budget of the stair interiors, and a shop is never given the stair interior.
- **Rooms.** Each shop takes its shopfront as for its sign (above). When two or more shops have their shopfront on the wall of the main door, a partition wall `PARTITION_M` (0.2 m, in `detail/interiors.py`) thick runs across the room, perpendicular to that wall, between each pair of neighbouring shops: on the bay boundary between their shopfronts, the middle one when other bays lie between. It stays in the pier, at least `min_pier_m / 2` from both openings. A shop belongs to the room its shopfront opens into; a shop left without a shopfront (more shops than bays) belongs to the room of the main door. A shop whose shopfront is on another wall does not split the room and shares the room that wall belongs to. The room of the main door keeps it; any other room with a shop gets a walkable `interior_door` (1.2 × 2.4 m) centred on its first shop's shopfront bay, in place of that shopfront, and that shop's sign goes on the door (a door gets no vinyl). Every room gets its walls, floor, ceiling, colours and light. Room ids are `<building>/L0` (the room of the main door), then `<building>/L0-1`, `<building>/L0-2`, … Measured on the test street (`tests/integration/test_shop_interiors.py`): an 18 × 12 m building with a bakery and a pharmacy is two rooms of 98 m², two doors, two lights and 104 triangles.
- **Courtyards.** A footprint with an inner ring keeps it as a hole in the room: inner walls run round the courtyard `wall_thickness_m` from it, facing into the room, the floor and the ceiling slab leave the hole out, and the band of wall round the courtyard is solid in the colliders. Windows on the courtyard side (only with `exposure.courtyard_openings`) are treated like the street ones. A light whose room centroid falls in the courtyard moves to a point of the room. Measured: a 24 × 24 m shop round an 8 × 8 m courtyard is one 474 m² room, 23 see-through shopfronts and 174 triangles. The stair rule still excludes courtyards; an `include_ids` building with one now gets its stair interior round it.
- **Several parts.** A footprint of several parts (a multipolygon with two outer rings) has its main room in the part that holds the main door. Every other part whose inner ring holds a shopfront is one more room. Two parts never share a wall (they would be one polygon), so a part is entered only through a door of its own: it gets one when one of the building's shops has its shopfront there (the rule above), and otherwise it is seen through its see-through windows only, with `reachable: false` on its room record and no door connection. A part with no shopfront stays a closed volume and keeps its glass `glass_dark`. Measured: a 12 × 10 m part with a café and an unreachable 10 × 8 m part are two rooms (107 and 70 m²), one door, two lights and 118 triangles; a third part 3.4 m square, too narrow for a shopfront, stays solid with its dark windows. The stair interior still refuses several parts (`footprint_not_a_simple_polygon`).
- **See-through windows.** Every display window, window or balcony door below the ceiling becomes a hole through the wall with one quad, as for the stair interiors (below). A window or a balcony door keeps `glass_view` (the framed window image) and its `<id>#glass` pane collider. A display window (`shopfront`) is `shop_glass`: frameless glass, one flat pale tint at 20 % alpha (`materials/openings.py`, RGB 196, 212, 220), and **no collider**: the wall collider is open on the whole pane above the stallriser, so a shot from either side crosses it. The adapter draws `shop_glass` with the SDK's glass material when the Unity project has one (an asset named `GlassVfx`, then `Glass`, under `Assets/OnwardCustomContent`; `docs/12-export.md`), and otherwise with `PP_Sprite_Lit` and the tint, both faces, so the player sees in from the street and out from the room. Vinyls stay on that glass (above). The interior record has `open_shopfronts` (the `shop_glass` openings) beside `glass_panes`; `through_windows` is their sum. Measured on a Murcia r150 run (37.98407, −1.12863, `run --no-unity`): 40 shop interiors, 297 see-through openings, 271 of them `shop_glass` and 26 `glass_view` windows; the 271 panes that are gone were 12 collision triangles each (3 252 of the shops' 45 120), the drawn triangles do not change (one quad either way), and 142 of the 200 vinyls are decals. A Madrid r150 run (40.40657, −3.71919) has 82 shopfronts but no shop interior (one `shop_too_large`, two `inner_space_too_small`), so all its glass stays `glass_dark` with strips.
- **Walking through a display window.** Nothing stops a body above the 0.25 m stallriser either: the stallriser stays solid in the wall collider, but it is under the 0.3 m agent step, and the Onward guide documents no player-only collision layer, so none is invented. What lies behind the opening is the shop room, which is closed (walls, partitions, ceiling, roof slab or the solid above), so a player who steps through lands in the shop and cannot leave the map that way. Bots do not path through: the navmesh cuts every footprint (`docs/11-onward-sdk.md`). Whether the Onward player can step over 0.25 m is not known; it is a line of the headset checklist (`quest_acceptance.md`), not a verified behaviour.
- **Colour.** Each room's walls and floor take the colours of its shops' category; the ceiling stays `interior_wall`. The category is that of the first shop of the room, in `facade.shops` order, whose tags match one in `interiors.shops.categories`; categories are tried in their listed order. A shop that matches none is `generic`. A room with no shop (a part seen through its windows) takes the category of every shop of the building. With one room this is the building's first matching shop, as before. Six shared albedo-only materials, not one per shop:

  | Category | Tags | Walls | Floor |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | `bakery` | `shop` bakery, pastry, confectionery | `interior_shop_bakery` (warm yellow) | `interior_shop_tile` |
  | `pharmacy` | `amenity=pharmacy`, `shop=chemist`, `healthcare=pharmacy` | `interior_shop_pharmacy` (pale green) | `interior_shop_tile` |
  | `bar` | `amenity` bar, pub, cafe, restaurant, fast_food, ice_cream | `interior_shop_bar` (terracotta) | `interior_shop_wood` |
  | `generic` | any other shop | `interior_shop_generic` (peach) | `interior_shop_wood` |

  The names must start with `interior_`, so the export bakes them in the interior lightmap class (`docs/12-export.md`). `materials apply` gives each one a photograph from the imported library (`SHOP_INTERIOR_TEXTURES` in `materials/apply.py`), repeating every 2 m, albedo only: the four wall colours share Poly Haven `plastered_wall` (`ph_plastered_wall`) in greyscale under their own tint, so the category still reads; `interior_shop_tile` is ambientCG `Tiles139` and `interior_shop_wood` Poly Haven `wood_planks` (`ph_wood_planks`), both in colour under a white base colour. Six materials and three textures for the whole map, however many shops: the walls' images are byte-identical, so the GLB keeps one. `materials_report.json` lists them under `textures.shop_interior` (library id, mode, tile, licence, SHA-256). Before this change they were flat colours over the plain procedural tile of `dress style`.
- **Light.** One light anchor in each room (ADR-013), reachable or not. See the cap below.
- **Room size** ([ADR-019](decisions/ADR-019-shop-rooms.md)). The room is the piece of the inset part (the part less `wall_thickness_m`) that the door opens into. Where a neck of the footprint is narrower than two walls (common in Catastro sub-buildings, ADR-016) the inset falls apart; the other pieces stay solid in the colliders and their windows stay `glass_dark` (`interior_window_omitted`). A footprint above `interiors.shops.max_area_m2` (600 m²) has its room cut to the band `interiors.shops.band_depth_m` (12 m) deep behind the wall of the main door, measured from the outer face, and closed by a back wall; the record has `room_depth_m`. Further parts with a shopfront still get their own room. A room under `interiors.shops.min_room_area_m2` (9 m²) is refused (`inner_space_too_small`); the stair interiors keep 20 m² (`MIN_ROOM_M2`). Before this, a shop above 600 m² was refused (`shop_too_large`) and so was any shop whose inset was not one polygon or under 20 m². Measured with the default configuration (no core): Madrid (40.40657, −3.719187, r150, Catastro) went from 0 to 3 shop interiors (one 579 m² band room, two necked sub-buildings) and 12 to 15 lights; Murcia (37.98407, −1.12863, r150) from 40 to 49 shop interiors, the 9 `inner_space_too_small` and 4 `shop_too_large` refusals gone, with 4 shops now turned down by `shops.max_triangles` (5 006 to 5 980 of 6 000 drawn triangles) and the 24 lights still full. Stair interiors did not change on either map.
- **Budget.** `interiors.shops.max_triangles` (6 000) caps the visual triangles of all shop interiors together, apart from `interiors.max_triangles`. A shop that does not fit is not built (`interior_not_generated`, info, `triangle_budget: … of shops.max_triangles …`) and keeps its glazed `shop_door`. Shops are tried nearest the map centre (the local origin) first. A footprint above `interiors.shops.max_area_m2` (600 m²) gets a smaller room, not none (*Room size*, below). Every room of a shop counts in its visual and collision budgets. Measured on the test block: the bakery in building 312 (222 m² of room, 8 see-through windows) is 64 triangles: two per floor and ceiling, the rest the walls around the door and window cuts. A 107 m² shop with 3 windows on the row block is 34. So the default budget holds about a hundred shops. The colliders add the cut walls, the panes and one solid above the ceiling: 108–2 828 collision triangles a shop on the Madrid and Murcia samples (*Triangle budget* below), about ten for each drawn triangle. `interiors.shops.max_collision_triangles` (72 000) caps them as `interiors.max_collision_triangles` does for the stair interiors.
- **Record.** The interior record has `kind: shop` (the stair interiors have `kind: residential`), `shop` (`category`, `wall_material`, `floor_material`, `attribute_origin: generated`; that of the room of the main door), `visual_triangles` and `collision_triangles`. Each shop room in `rooms` also has `reachable` (whether it has a door), `shops` (indices into `facade.shops`) and its own `shop`. `connections` has one `door` per reachable room. `counts.shop_interiors`, `counts.shop_interior_visual_triangles` and `counts.shop_interior_collision_triangles` in `detail_report.json` list them. A shop interior also keeps `panoramax apply` off that facade, as any interior does.
- **Light cap.** Shop rooms and stair-interior rooms share `interiors.lights_max` (160, at most 256), the same number as the adapter's `build.interior_lights_max` (`docs/12-export.md`). When the map has more rooms, shop rooms come first, then every room by the distance of its light from the local origin (the map centre), then by room id. A room past the cap keeps its probes, loses `light_m` and `range_m`, and gets `"light": "over_cap"`. Each such room is an `interior_light_dropped` issue (info), and `counts.interior_lights` is `{"max", "placed", "dropped": [room ids]}`.

Before this change a shop building could get the stair interior when it matched the rule (building 312 on the test block did: three floors and a stair behind a shopfront), and every other shop was a closed volume behind dark glass.

#### Furnishing (on by default, ADR-031)

At most four pieces per shop room (`max_pieces_per_room`). The rooms are furnished after every building is drawn, nearest the map centre (the centre of the playable core) first, so the map budget goes to the shops the players can reach; whole rooms or none, as before.

On by default since 10 November 2026: `interiors.shops.furnish.enabled` is `true` in `config/detail.default.json` (the maintainer's decision, before the headset session; ADR-031 amendment). A configuration without the `furnish` block, or with `enabled: false`, plans nothing, reads no catalogue, and writes every file as before furnishing existed. The default sets mix Kenney, Poly Haven and Quaternius Ultimate Home Interior rows (`assets/quaternius_home_interior`), lighter pieces (68–600 triangles) that let more rooms fit `max_triangles`; the set order does not matter, since the planner starts at a seeded row of the id-sorted set. When this machine has local Asset Store catalogues (`assets/asset_store_*`, [ADR-035](decisions/ADR-035-local-asset-store-catalogues.md)), `load_detail_config` adds their rows that carry a `furnish` hint to the matching sets, at most two per slot, lightest first; `ONWARD_LOCAL_CATALOGUES=0` leaves them out. [ADR-031](decisions/ADR-031-shop-furnishing.md) is proposed until the headset checks in `docs/09-perf.md` have run.

**Semantic sets and zones (default since 10 November 2026, [ADR-036](decisions/ADR-036-semantic-library-and-zoned-furnishing.md)).** Both furnishing blocks have `selection: semantic`. When the configuration loads, `detail/recipes.py` fills each shop type's set (generic, bakery, bar, pharmacy) and the home set from the `semantic` block every library row carries (`overgrow/semantics.py`; `tools/describe_library.py` rewrites the manifests). A set then has, besides `counter`, `wall` and `decor`, the zone slots `wall_deep`, `center`, `table`, `seat`, `corner`, `rug` and `top`, and the planner places them by zones:

- a rug, then the counter and its decor;
- deep wall pieces, then corners;
- wall runs (one model per edge);
- table groups with their seats and one thing on top;
- island rows with an aisle;
- the walls again.

The cap is one piece per 8 m² (at least 4, at most `max_pieces_per_room`: 8 in a shop since plan P0, 12 in a home). Decor, tops, rugs and seats are left out of the cap. A slot no row fills keeps the hand-written entries, and a set with no zone slot keeps the plan below exactly. `ONWARD_SEMANTIC_SETS=0` keeps the hand-written sets. Budgets: shops 45 000 triangles (70 000 before plan P0, 12 November 2026), 8 000 collision, 64 assets; homes 30 000, 4 000, 64. The record's `slot` takes the new names.

`detail` plans catalogue furniture in each reachable shop room after the room is built and charged (`detail/furnish.py`); `overgrow place` builds it (`docs/01-pipeline.md`, 8c). Every piece is `attribute_origin: generated`, and the rows' semantics are `estimated` (no person reviewed a rendered view).

- **Rows.** Only `placement: interior` rows of `catalogues` (`kenney_food`, `kenney_furniture`, `kenney_mini_shop`, `polyhaven_shop`; all CC0 1.0, `docs/legal/data-sources.md`), read as overgrow reads them: opaque materials only, the load-time level of detail applied, so `detail` prices what `overgrow` builds. Refused at load (`DetailConfigError`): a set key that is not a category or `generic`, a row outside those catalogues or not `interior`, the floor, wall, fence and column tiles, `character_employee`, `mannequin`, the basket, the cart and the hand truck, a counter deeper than 1.0 m (`MAX_COUNTER_DEPTH_M`) or a wall piece deeper than 0.8 m (`MAX_WALL_DEPTH_M`) across its wall after its turn, and decor outside `kenney_food`. These checks read the manifests only when `enabled` is true. A row the interior loader refuses (a transparent material) or that is not installed stops `detail`.
- **Sets.** `sets.<category>.{counter, wall, decor}` by the room's shop category (*Colour* above: OSM `shop=*`/`amenity=*`, the Catastro use, or an Overture place), `generic` for any other. An entry is an id or `{id, turn_deg}` (0, 90, 180, 270). The default sets use Kenney Furniture rows and Poly Haven `Shelf_01`; bakery and bar decor is Food Kit bread, cups and bottles.
- **Floor and keep-outs.** A piece stands inside its room polygon less `wall_gap_m` (0.03 m), so off the outer walls, the partitions and a courtyard. None enters the room's door zone (`door_clearance_m` deep, 0.3 m wider than the door each side, as for the stairs) or a `pane_clear_m` (0.15 m) band behind a see-through opening.
- **Counter.** One, back to the wall of the room's display windows (the ring edge with the most `shop_glass`, else the wall of its door), as near the windows as it fits, else on the walls either side of it. Without a spot the room records `no_counter_spot` and keeps its wall pieces. **Decor:** at most two rows of the decor slot on the counter top, visual only.
- **Wall pieces.** Back to the wall at the first free stretch of an edge of any ring, `piece_gap_m` (0.1 m) apart, one piece per edge per round, longest edge first, up to `max_pieces_per_room` (8) pieces in all, counter and decor included. No piece is taller than the ceiling less `head_clear_m` (0.1 m).
- **Clear path.** Every floor piece keeps a front zone `walk_width_m` (1.2 m, schema minimum) deep across its length, inside the room and free of other pieces. After each piece the room less the pieces, eroded by `walk_width_m / 2`, must keep every place the empty room reached joined to the door zone and reach every front zone. A piece that breaks either is refused (`blocks_path`). An erosion, not the navmesh: the bots' navmesh cuts every footprint (`docs/11-onward-sdk.md`).
- **Choice.** `stable_unit(seed, building, room, slot, ring, edge, k)` picks the first row to try in the set sorted by id, so neither the order of a set nor of a catalogue changes the plan. Two runs with one seed give byte-identical `scene_detail.json`.
- **Facing.** `yaw_deg` turns the model's local −Y (glTF +Z, the usual front) into the room, after `turn_deg`. An assumption for the headset check.
- **Colliders.** A piece at least 0.5 m tall and 0.4 m on its short side (the overgrow cover rule, `is_solid`) is `solid`: `overgrow` writes one box for it, 12 collision triangles, in the collision GLB only. Decor never is.
- **Budget.** `interiors.shops.furnish.max_triangles` (12 000), `max_collision_triangles` (1 200) and `max_assets` (16 distinct rows a map), apart from `shops.max_triangles`, which furniture never spends. A room is furnished whole or not at all, in the order the shop interiors were built (nearest the map centre first). A room that does not fit keeps its shop interior and gets `shop_not_furnished` (info): `triangle_budget: needs N triangles, M left of shops.furnish.max_triangles L; room <id>`, `collision_budget: …`, `asset_cap: …`, `unreachable` (no door), `blocks_path` (no piece kept a path), or `no_free_wall`.
- **Light.** The probe columns of a furnished room keep out of the pieces' plan (`light_anchors(..., furniture=)`); the light does not move.
- **Record.** A furnished room in the interior record's `rooms` gains `furnishing`: `method: shop_furnishing`, `attribute_origin: generated`, `set`, `pieces` (each `id` `furnish/<building>/<n>`, `asset_id`, `catalogue`, `licence`, `role`, `slot` (`counter`, `wall`, `decor`), `position_m` (the footprint centre at the base of the piece), `yaw_deg`, `turn_deg`, `size_m`, `footprint_m`, `triangles`, `solid`, `glb_sha256`, `attribute_origin: generated`, `semantics_origin: estimated`), `visual_triangles`, `collision_triangles` and `refusals` (pieces the room did not get: `no_counter_spot`, `blocks_path`). `detail_report.json` gains `counts.shop_furnishing` only when it is on: `shop_rooms`, `reachable_rooms`, `furnished_rooms`, `pieces`, `pieces_by_asset`, `refusals_by_reason` (rooms), `piece_refusals_by_reason`, `visual_triangles`, `max_triangles`, `collision_triangles`, `max_collision_triangles`, `assets`, `max_assets` and `materials_added`. Both are additions; with the option off neither appears.
- **Measured** (7 November 2026, copies of the bench run `20261107T170959Z`, option on, every local stage through `export`): `bench_murcia` 7 of 27 reachable rooms furnished, 56 pieces, 11 454 triangles, 41 colliders, 20 rooms turned down by the budget and 3 unreachable; `bench_madrid` 7 of 70, 53 pieces, 11 822, 43 colliders, 63 turned down; `bench_krakow` 4 of 4, 32 pieces, 5 680, 25 colliders. The shop interiors (24, 67, 4), their triangles, the lightmap pages and the 32 layout routes do not change; the package draws go from 213 to 247, 504 to 547 and 272 to 293. On the test street the bakery room of building 312 gets 8 pieces (`tests/integration/test_shop_furnishing.py`).

### Simplified interiors

Generated in **every building of up to five storeys** that is not a shop and has room for one. The count is `sha256(seed, interiors, quota)` from `min_buildings` through `max_buildings`, both 1 000 by default, so the rule decides. A map with fewer matches builds only those:

- `include_ids` first, except `exclude_ids`;
- then buildings that match the rule (`interiors.rule`): every category but `religious`, 1 to 5 storeys as the facade draws them (`min_levels`, `max_levels`), 20–2 000 m², not a landmark, one polygon. A courtyard stays a hole in the room. Ordered by `sha256(seed:interior:id)`.

Buildings are tried in that order until the count is reached. A building whose plan fails does not use up the count, and `interior_not_generated` (info) gives the reason: `no_street_frontage` (no wall to the street, so nowhere for a door: a part inside a block), `no_door_position`, `no_room_behind_door`, `inner_space_too_small`, `no_room_for_stair`. The objects and issues are still written in building order.

**Triangle budget.** `max_triangles` (default 24 000) caps the visual triangles of all `interior/<building>` nodes together: walls, floors, slab soffits and stairs. An interior that would take the total over it is not built. The building keeps the facade it has with interiors off, and `interior_not_generated` (info) records `triangle_budget` with the cost and what was left. Later buildings in the queue are still tried, so a smaller interior can fit after a larger one was turned down. The total is `counts.interior_visual_triangles` in `detail_report.json`.

The drawn triangles are counted before anything else is built. The interior is planned and its visual meshes are built (`interior_visual`); only when they fit are the shell with the interior door and the colliders built (`assemble_interior`). A building turned down costs its plan and its visual meshes, and its shell is built once, with the plain door. Before this change every candidate was built whole, shell and colliders included, and then thrown away when it did not fit, so a map with every match allowed built and discarded dozens of shells once the budget was full. The selection is the same: the count is the same number, taken earlier. `tests/integration/test_interior_budget.py` runs the old build-then-count loop beside the new one on the three test blocks, under budgets that turn interiors down, and compares every record, mesh and issue. On the Madrid and Murcia samples below, with every match allowed (`max_buildings` 50), the manifest and the report are byte for byte the same apart from the new collision fields, and `detail_scene` takes about 18% less time on Madrid (6.5 s to 5.3 s, median of three, a shared machine): 75 buildings there are turned down by the budgets, 71 of them shops.

**Collision budget.** `max_collision_triangles` (default 72 000) caps the collision triangles of the stair interiors together, and `interiors.shops.max_collision_triangles` (72 000) those of the shop interiors. An interior counts its own colliders: the cut wall bands, the slabs, the stair ramps, the roof deck or the solid above, and the glass panes. The building's balcony boxes and parapet are not counted. The collision count is known only once the colliders are built, so an interior over this limit is turned down after it was built, with `triangle_budget: needs N collision triangles, M left of max_collision_triangles L` (or `shops.max_collision_triangles`), and its building is rebuilt as with interiors off. The totals are `counts.interior_collision_triangles` and `counts.shop_interior_collision_triangles`; each interior record has `collision_triangles`. The collision limits are separate from `max_triangles`, not added to it: a collider costs physics, not drawing, the scene budget already keeps the two apart (`max_visual_triangles`, `max_collision_triangles`), and counting about 1.3 k collision triangles a stair interior against the 8 000 drawn would leave three interiors on a map that has fifteen. The defaults sit above everything measured, so the drawn limits still bind first and no map changes its interiors:

Stairs are most of an interior: a flight is a closed stepped solid of about 130 triangles. Eight interiors of 2–3 floors give 16–24 furnished rooms, within the adapter's 24 baked lights (ADR-013).

Each interior has a through-door on the main facade, a straight stair (tread 0.28 m, riser ≤ 0.185 m) between accessible floors, and partitions with a passage. The manifest describes rooms and connections (`door`, `stair`). If there is no room, `interior_not_generated` (info) records the reason, for example `no_room_for_stair`.

`floors` is the furnished count (default 3, and never more than the storeys minus one, except that a one-storey building has its room under the roof). A building that can hold three floors gets three when `sha256(seed, id, interior_floors) < 0.5`, and two otherwise.

**The roof comes first.** Further flights continue from the top furnished floor to the roof deck. They take turns in two wells, because a flight standing right above the one below it fills the head room of that flight's top steps and nobody can climb it. The second well is the one of the last storey flight; a building with no storey flight needs a free one. Each storey they pass is an empty floor between the two wells, lined from inside: an `interior_wall` band on every ring of the interior runs from the top furnished ceiling to the underside of the roof slab, because the shell faces outward only and would otherwise show the street through the wall from those flights. The room windows of the furnished floors stay below it. The deck is the flat roof with that well cut out; where the template has a parapet, the parapet is a collider, and a flat roof without one has an open edge. When the well of the climb does not fit beside the wells of the furnished floors, the building gives up furnished floors, down to the ground floor alone, before it gives up the roof. It has no climb when no well fits (a footprint under about 7 m long has no room for a flight), when it would need a second well and has room for one only, when the height is not a whole number of storeys from the top floor, or when the roof is pitched: a hip or a tagged `roof:shape` is left closed. `roof_access` on the record says which. A building of three storeys or more with no room for any stair is `no_room_for_stair`; one or two storeys keep the room without a stair.

Walls, floors, slab soffits, and stairs use `interior_wall`, `interior_floor`, and `stair`, which `materials apply` paints from the texture plan. The roof deck keeps the roof photograph.

Windows, shopfronts, and balcony doors below the accessible ceiling keep a hole through the exterior wall and the inner wall, and one glass quad in that hole. The quad is `glass_view`: a single piece, no flipped copy, and no renderer collider. The same opening is cut out of the concrete wall collider, through the full wall thickness, and one closed pane is written on a separate collision node `<id>#glass`. The pane is 2 cm thick, centred in the wall, and inset 1 cm from the jambs. The adapter assigns `PP_Sprite_Lit` and the window PNG on `_MainTex`. That shader is already transparent and draws both faces, so the pane is visible from the street and from the room. The dark fill of the window image is about 0.35 alpha; the frame and mullions stay opaque. The image is RGBA and is not a tile of the opaque atlas. `PP_Opaque_Simple_Lit` still covers windows above the accessible ceiling, buildings without an interior, and `glass_boarded`. Those stay solid concrete: they are not openings. The stone frame, the sill, the header, and the pier stay concrete. A window that does not land on the inner wall is omitted (`interior_window_omitted`, info). The counts are `through_windows` and `omitted_windows` on the interior record. A shop's display window is the exception: `shop_glass`, no frame, no pane collider (*Shop interiors*), so `glass_panes` plus `open_shopfronts` matches `through_windows`; a stair interior has no shopfront and its `glass_panes` matches `through_windows`. Transparency on Quest is a known cost and has not been measured.

#### Floor furnishing (on by default, ADR-034)

On by default since 10 November 2026: `interiors.furnish.enabled` is `true` in `config/detail.default.json` (ADR-034 amendment). A configuration without the block, or with `enabled: false`, plans nothing, reads no catalogue, and writes every file as before. The `generic` set has twelve wall pieces, ten of them Quaternius (`kenney_furniture` and `quaternius_home_interior`): no more distinct rows than `max_assets` (12), so variety alone never refuses a room as `asset_cap`. [ADR-034](decisions/ADR-034-residential-floor-furnishing.md) is proposed until the headset checks in `docs/09-perf.md` have run.

The same planner as *Furnishing* above (ADR-031), on each floor record `<building>/L<n>` of a built stair interior (`kind: residential`), after the interior is charged. `overgrow place` builds the pieces at the floor's height. The interior stays `representative_of_real_interior: false`: the furniture is generic, `attribute_origin: generated`, `semantics_origin: estimated`, and says nothing about the real dwelling.

- **Block.** `interiors.furnish`, the shape of `interiors.shops.furnish`, with one set, `generic`, of `wall` pieces: `counter` and `decor` must be empty, and another set key is refused by the schema. The rows pass the shop checks (an allowed catalogue, `placement: interior`, not a tile, character or clutter row, at most 0.8 m deep after their turn). The default set holds Kenney Furniture bookcases, TV cabinets, a side table with drawers and a desk; beds and sofas are too deep for a wall piece.
- **Floor.** Every floor is the interior's one polygon (`plan.inner`) at the record's `floor_z_m`, under its `ceiling_z_m`; a piece stands at `floor_z_m` and is no taller than the ceiling less `head_clear_m`.
- **Entry.** The door zone on the ground floor; on each floor above, the arrival landing of the flight from below (`interiors.flight_landings`: `stair.landing_m` long, as wide as the stair, against the short side of the well, as the stair placement checked it).
- **Stairs.** The wells of the flights that stand on the floor and of the flights from the level below are holes in its walk region, taken by level (the roof climb's reused well counts only on the levels it touches). No piece in a well plus 0.3 m (`WELL_CLEARANCE_M`), in the foot landing of a flight that starts on the floor or the arrival landing of the flight that ends on it, roof climb included (`stair_zone`), and no front zone over a well.
- **Windows.** A `pane_clear_m` band behind each see-through window whose sill is on that storey (`floor_z_m <= v0 < ceiling_z_m`).
- **Clear path.** As for the shops, and the walk region (the floor less its wells and the pieces, eroded by `walk_width_m / 2`) must also keep every stair foot of the floor joined to its entry. A landing lies between the well and the walls, so the entry and the feet count as reached within `walk_width_m / 2`. A floor whose empty plan already fails is refused whole (`blocks_path`).
- **Budget.** Its own: `interiors.furnish.max_triangles` (12 000), `max_collision_triangles` (1 200), `max_assets` (12), `max_pieces_per_room` (6), all or nothing per floor, in the order the interiors were built. It never spends `interiors.max_triangles`, the shop budgets or `shops.furnish`. A floor left bare keeps its interior and gets `floor_not_furnished` (info): `triangle_budget: needs N triangles, M left of interiors.furnish.max_triangles L; room <id>`, `collision_budget: …`, `asset_cap: …`, `blocks_path`, or `no_free_wall`.
- **Light.** Each floor keeps its ADR-013 light (`light_m`, `range_m`). The probe columns of a furnished floor keep out of that floor's pieces only (`light_anchors(..., room_furniture=)`); `cap_lights` and `lights_max` do not change.
- **Record.** A furnished floor record gains `furnishing` in the shop shape with `method: residential_furnishing` and `set: generic`; piece ids `furnish/<building>/<n>` run on across the building's floors. `detail_report.json` gains `counts.floor_furnishing` (the keys of `counts.shop_furnishing`, with `floor_rooms` in place of `shop_rooms`) only when the option is on.

### Street elements

- **Observed:** OSM POIs `tree`, `bollard`, `bicycle_parking`, `bus_stop`, `fire_hydrant`, `traffic_signals`, `street_lamp`, `bench`, `waste_basket`. Marked `attribute_origin: observed`.
- **Generated:** lamps every 25 m beside `vehicular` ways, 0.5 m from the kerb, at least 1 m from buildings and 8 m from each other. Marked `attribute_origin: generated`.
- **Street lamps, observed** (plan Q6, 11 November 2026). An OSM `street_lamp` within 0.5 m of a facade, or tagged `lamp_mount=wall` or `support=wall_mounted`/`wall`, hangs on the nearest facade point as a `wall_lamp` (`placement_rule: osm_poi_on_the_nearest_facade`): a bracket and a lantern from 3.1 to 3.75 m, local +x out of the wall, no collider, 32 triangles (a pole lamp has 40). Paris maps its wall lamps on the facade line: on Rue Mouffetard 107 of the 116 lamps that found no free ground stood within 0.5 m of one. Every lamp, observed or generated, keeps the 8 m of `generated_street_lamps.min_spacing_m` from those already placed, in id order: one square there had twelve poles within 30 × 37 m. A free-standing observed lamp reaches its arm away from the nearest facade instead of east. The night street lights (`perf layout`) hang under a wall lamp's lantern too, 0.57 m out.
- An element is placed only when its footprint fits on free walkable ground and does not touch another element. A missed observed element warns `urban_element_not_placed`. A missed generated element (lamp, bench or bin: its id holds `#`), and a lamp kept off its neighbour by the spacing, record the same code as info: neither loses observed data. On Rue Mouffetard the warnings of `detail` went from 258 to 43. A generated lamp, bench or bin also keeps off the ground in front of every ground-floor door (`door`, `shop_door`, `landmark_door`, and `interior_door`, the door of a shop room): 1.5 m out from the wall and 0.3 m past each jamb (`DOOR_CLEAR_M`, `DOOR_SIDE_CLEAR_M` in `detail/scene.py`). A refused one gives the reason `in_front_of_a_door`. An observed element stays where OSM puts it, because the doors are generated. On Barcelona Sarria a generated bench stood 0.47 m in front of a shop door, across its whole width.
- Each type is one shared prototype mesh, instanced with a glTF `matrix`, plus a collision box.

### Playable core and scenic ring

`--core-radius-m` (ADR-017; `run` and the `next:` line from `generate` read `core_radius_m` from `map.json`) sets a square of that half-side around the map centre. Without it every building is detailed as above.

- **Tier.** A building whose footprint shares area with the core is `core`; the rest are `scenic`. A footprint that only touches the edge is scenic. Each building record gets `tier`; so do its interior, its landmark towers and its `building:part` volumes, which take the tier of their building.
- **Core.** Everything in this document applies, with two differences: the interior queue holds core buildings only, so the quota and the triangle budgets go to buildings a player can reach, and `counts.facade_uses` and `counts.wall_materials` count core buildings only. A stair interior or a shop room also needs the whole footprint inside the core: past the edge the ground colliders end and the perimeter wall stands, so a room there would have no floor and a stair there could not be reached (Madrid r300 had three such flights). Such a building keeps its core facade and its shopfront; `interior_not_generated` (info) says `crosses_core_edge`, also for an `include_ids` entry.
- **Scenic shell.** The footprint extruded from 0 to the building height: one wall per edge and a flat roof cap, no base. No openings, doors, balconies, parapets, hip roofs, towers, interiors or shops, so `signs` draws no shop sign or vinyl for it; `signs` also hangs no street plate on a wall whose midpoint is outside the core. No collider (`collider.source: none_scenic`). The wall is one of `scenic_wall_light`, `scenic_wall_warm` and `scenic_wall_ochre`, by `sha256(seed, building, "scenic")`; the roof is `roof_flat_grey`. The record keeps the facade use and says `facade.template: scenic_shell`, `ground_floor: none`, `door: none`. A landmark keeps `needs_custom_model: true`. A scenic `building:part` keeps its visual and loses its collider.
- **Street elements** whose position is outside the core are dropped, observed or generated; `counts.tiers.scenic.urban_elements_dropped` counts them.
- **Ground colliders** (`collider/walkable`, `collider/carriageway`, `collider/walkable_crossing`) are rebuilt as closed slabs over the part of their polygon inside the core, with `clipped_to_core: true`. The visual ground, curbs and markings stay over the whole site.
- **Report.** `scene_detail.json` gets `core: {half_side_m, buildings: {core, scenic}}` (`null` without a core; a budget core adds its fields, below). `counts.tiers` gives, per tier, `buildings`, `visual_triangles` and `collision_triangles` of the building objects (shells, interiors, towers, parts). `run` prints them on the `detail` line.

Measured on Madrid (40.40657, −3.719187) with Catastro: see [09-perf.md](09-perf.md#playable-core).

#### Hidden walls

A core building's exposed wall that faces no ground a player reaches is laid out plain (plan P0, [ADR-017 amendment of 12 November 2026](decisions/ADR-017-playable-core.md)): flat windows on every storey, no balcony, no shopfront; its main door stays, and an interior does not pierce its windows. The reached ground is the largest island of walkable ground of the whole map (`detail/reach.py`, the rule `perf layout` bakes for the navmesh), and a wall is hidden when the ground 1 m in front of its middle lies more than 6 m from it: a closed block's courtyard, an alley no street joins. A map without a core is unchanged.

#### Intermediate ring

`--mid-ring-m W` (with `--core-radius-m`; `map.json` `mid_ring_m`, see [02-configuration.md](02-configuration.md#one-folder-per-map)) adds a third tier between the core and the scenic ring ([ADR-017 amendment of 9 November 2026](decisions/ADR-017-playable-core.md#amendment-9-november-2026-an-intermediate-hlod-ring-plan-d3)). It is off by default until a headset session validates it; without it nothing changes, byte for byte.

- **Tier.** `tiers.ring_tier`: the core is decided as above; a building that is not core and shares area with the square of half-side `core.half_side_m + W` is `mid`, one that only touches its outer edge is `scenic`. Whole buildings only; `building:part` volumes follow their building and lose their collider. With a budget core the band follows the solved half-side, and N counts only the core.
- **Mid shell.** The footprint extruded to its height in its scenic wall material, the roof form its roof plan gives (an OSM `roof:shape`, the estimated or template hip, the nDSM gable with `lidar_roofs`, else a flat cap; no parapet) and painted windows: ADR-025 flat openings (a hole and a `glass_dark` quad, 1.2 × 1.4 m, 0.9 m sill, one per 3 m bay) on exposed walls, a whole wall-storey row at a time, longest wall first, at most 150 visual triangles per building. A roof that would go over is drawn flat (`roof.fallback: mid_triangle_cap`); a footprint whose plain shell is already over 150 gets no window. No doors, balconies, parapets, towers, interiors, shops or collider (`collider.source: none_mid`); a landmark gets no window.
- **Record.** `tier: mid`, `facade.template` and `representation` `mid_shell`, `facade.storeys`, `facade.mid {windows, exposed_faces, cap}`, `facade.materials` with the roof's origin and source (an Overture roof colour can apply to a mid roof, not to its wall), `checks.facade_open_edges`; `roof.origin` is the roof plan's. `materials apply` maps the walls and windows of a mid node onto `scenic_trim_<style>`.
- **Report.** `scene_detail.json` `core` gains `mid_ring_m` and `buildings.mid`; `counts.tiers.mid` gives `buildings`, `visual_triangles`, `collision_triangles` (0) and `triangles_per_building {mean, min, max}` of the shells. `run` prints the mid tier on the `detail` line. `counts.lidar_roofs.core_buildings` counts the mid buildings too, since their roofs are classified.

#### Budget core

`--core-target-triangles N` (with `--core-radius-m M` as the largest half-side; `map.json` `core`, see [02-configuration.md](02-configuration.md#one-folder-per-map)) picks the half-side from a triangle target instead ([ADR-017 amendment of 7 November 2026](decisions/ADR-017-playable-core.md#amendment-7-november-2026-budget-mode)). It is off by default until a headset session validates it.

- **Probe.** `detail_scene` runs once with the core at M. A building's cost is the visual triangles of its core objects in that run: the building, its `building:part` volumes and its landmark towers. Its interior, stair or shop, is costed apart, since an interior needs the whole footprint inside the core and each kind has its own triangle budget (`interiors.max_triangles`, `interiors.shops.max_triangles`). An interior the probe refused for that budget (`interior_not_generated`, `triangle_budget: needs N triangles`) is costed at the N it needed, since a smaller core may build it.
- **Shape and seed.** The core stays the square around the map centre: spawns and routes are placed later, by `perf layout`. Each footprint has a reach, its L∞ distance from the centre (`tiers.core_reach`, 0 when it holds the centre), and an extent, the largest `max(|x|, |y|)` of its outline (`tiers.core_extent`). A building is core when its reach is under the half-side, the rule above; its interior counts when its extent is at most the half-side. Buildings are never split.
- **Solve.** `tiers.solve_core` tries 50 m, M, and the midpoint between every two neighbouring reaches or extents, so no footprint lies on the edge, and keeps the one whose prediction is nearest N, the smaller core on a tie. Under 50 m a building is always core; at or past M it never is. A target the largest core cannot reach keeps M.
- **Run.** `detail_scene` runs again at that half-side, unless it is M, where the probe is kept. Only that run's objects and issues are written; the output is the same as a run with `--core-radius-m` at that half-side, apart from the budget fields below.
- **Check.** `core` gains `mode: budget`, `target_triangles`, `max_half_side_m`, `predicted_triangles` (the model), `achieved_triangles` (`counts.tiers.core.visual_triangles` of the kept run), `tolerance` 0.05 and `within_tolerance`. `core_budget_floor` (info): the 50 m core is already over N. `core_budget_ceiling` (info): the core at M is under N. `core_budget_missed` (warning): neither, but the kept run is more than 5 % off N; this happens when one building is a large share of N, since the square grows a building at a time. No issue is critical and nothing is searched again.
- **What N counts.** `detail`'s core visual triangles. Dress, overgrow, signs, the ground and the SDK prefabs come on top of them in Unity; see [09-perf.md](09-perf.md#budget-core) for the measured ratio.
- **Cost.** Two `detail` runs. The cost per building is not strictly additive (party walls hidden by a neighbour that turns scenic, the interior queue, the shop order by distance), which is why the check reads the kept run and not the model.

### Collision

`scene_collision.glb` is a separate file, one material `collider`:

- buildings: the phase-03 prism (a flat facade; recesses and balconies do not change it), plus balcony boxes;
- buildings with an interior: walls up to the roof deck with a door hole and a hole for each `glass_view` and `shop_glass` opening, a separate `<id>#glass` pane in each `glass_view` hole (a `shop_glass` display window has none: a shot crosses it), a slab and a stair ramp for each flight, a roof-deck slab with the stair well cut out, and the parapet; without roof access, a solid above the furnished floors. Above the roof deck only the parapet stands: the walls once rose to the parapet top, so the partitions between shop rooms stood 0.7 m over the roof, drawn by nothing, and on the headset a player could not step out of a roof lift past one;
- street elements: instanced boxes;
- ground: the phase-03 slabs.

Every collider must be closed, manifold, and have positive volume (`collider_not_closed`, `interior_collider_not_closed`).

## LoD2 landmarks

Plan D6, opt-in, [ADR-008](decisions/ADR-008-landmark-volumes.md) amendment (proposed until the headset session, `docs/09-perf.md` *D6*). `detail --lod2-landmarks <lod2_index.json>` reads the block ZIPs a `landmark_mesh` source plugin downloaded: today `es.madrid_lod2`, which `run --source es.madrid_lod2` switches on for a Madrid map ([ADR-018](decisions/ADR-018-source-plugins.md), licence in `docs/legal/data-sources.md`). Without the flag nothing is read and every output is the same, byte for byte.

**The index** (`data/raw/madrid_lod2/lod2_index.json`, schema `onward_generator.lod2_landmarks` 1.0.0): `source`, `provider`, `license` (`CC-BY-4.0`), `attribution`, `data_date`, `crs` (`EPSG:25830`), `scene` (path and SHA-256 of the scene the candidates came from), `candidates` (the scene's landmark ids, by the `heights` rule) and `blocks`, each `{cod_manzan, path, sha256, size_bytes, url}`. `detail` refuses another schema, licence or CRS, a block whose SHA-256 differs, a missing block, a path that leaves the index folder, a ZIP with no or two `.obj` files, a member path with `..`, an OBJ over 32 MiB unzipped, 400 000 vertices or 800 000 triangles, an OBJ whose `# COORDINATE_SYSTEM` header does not end in EPSG 25830, and a malformed OBJ line (by number). A scene frame in another CRS is refused too: nothing is reprojected. Each refusal exits 1 before any output is written.

**Which buildings.** Only a landmark (`heights.json` `landmark`) of the core, with `landmarks.enabled`. A scenic or mid landmark keeps its shell; a core landmark with an interior keeps its placeholder (`landmark_lod2_not_used`).

**How** (`detail/lod2.py`, settings `Lod2Settings`, in code, not in `detail.json`):

1. Each OBJ `usemtl` group (one per building or part) is moved to the local grid: the frame's `origin_projected` is subtracted from the EPSG:25830 metres; x/y that look like degrees are refused.
2. Match: a group whose plan convex hull lies at least `min_group_overlap` (0.5) on the footprint is the landmark's; together the matched hulls must cover `min_footprint_cover` (0.6) of the footprint, which must be at least 20 m². Otherwise `landmark_lod2_no_match` (info) or `landmark_lod2_footprint_too_small` (warning), and the placeholder and tower stay.
3. Ground: the highest base of the outer wall columns (within 1 m of the footprint edge) is z 0, so no wall floats over a sloping street; lower vertices are raised to 0. A height over `landmarks.max_tower_height_m` gives `landmark_lod2_height_out_of_range`. A matched group whose top is not above that ground (a low annex on the downhill side) is left out with `landmark_lod2_group_below_ground` (warning) before the clip and the colliders, and the cover of the groups left is checked again against `min_footprint_cover` (`landmark_lod2_no_match` if it falls short).
4. Clip: a vertex up to `clip_tolerance_m` (1 m) outside moves onto the footprint edge. A triangle that still leaves the footprint is cut exactly at its edge: a sloped or flat one in plan, keeping its own plane; a wall along its plan line, in its own vertical plane. Each piece keeps the triangle's facing; a triangle with nothing on the footprint goes. Every edge the cut opened on the footprint edge is walled straight down to the street with two triangles facing the way the cut-away face did, so a group that reaches past the footprint ends in a closed face there instead of an open shell (the LoD2 groups' own open edges are left as the source has them). `clip.dropped_triangles`, `cut_triangles` and `closure_triangles` count each; `clip.outside_area_m2` checks that nothing is outside. The footprint is not changed. The part of the footprint that the kept triangles do not cover in plan (a gap between groups, the notch of an L-shaped group inside its convex hull) gets a floor at street level, so the ground cut out under the building is not open.
5. Cap: at most `max_triangles` (2000) visual triangles, by vertex clustering with a cell of 0.25 m doubled up to four times, then clipped and closed again. Over the cap, after the passes or after the second clip: `landmark_lod2_over_cap`, placeholder.
6. Materials: a triangle whose unit normal z is at least 0.35 takes `landmarks.roof_material`, the rest `landmarks.wall_material`. Each group's winding is turned so its normals face out. UVs are the generic surface ones (`uv_frame` is not set).
7. Colliders: one closed prism per matched group, its hull clipped to the footprint, from 0 to the group's top; the visual mesh is never a collider. A group with no closed prism gives `landmark_lod2_collider_not_closed` (critical). A `building:part` of the landmark is dropped: the volume holds it. A neighbour keeps its blank party wall against a landmark drawn with LoD2, in the party material: the shell height that would hide it is the placeholder's, and the LoD2 volume can be lower or leave that edge open.

**Record.** The building keeps its id, footprint, height and template fields. `facade.representation` becomes `observed_lod2`, `representative_of_real_volume: true`, `representative_of_real_facade: false`, `needs_custom_model: false`; `collider.source` is `lod2_group_prisms`; and a `lod2` block holds `provider`, `source`, `license`, `attribution`, `data_date`, `cod_manzan`, `obj_dates`, `objects` (`<COD_MANZAN>:<group>`), `geometry` (`origin: observed`), `match`, `ground_offset`, `clip` and `simplification` (each `origin: estimated`, with their parameters and the triangle counts before and after; `clip` also counts the cut, closure and floor triangles), `collider` and `materials`. `inputs.lod2_landmarks` is the index path, SHA-256 and source; `counts.lod2_landmarks` holds `matched`, the `landmark_lod2_*` issues by code, the visual and collision triangles of the matched landmarks and `building_parts_replaced`. When a landmark is drawn with it, the dataset's CC BY line joins the attribution, which `export package` copies to the package `attribution` list. The map description that `export package` builds from the same list is cut to the listing limit, and on bench_madrid the Madrid line falls past the cut, like several Overture lines.
